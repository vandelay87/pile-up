# Prototype v5 spec: over and through

This spec gathers every technical decision for prototype v5 in one place and ends in the ordered list of build tickets. v5 asks one question: **do enemies that beat walls in new ways (the jumper over them and onto buildings, the brute through them, and a crush tumbling onto the buildings behind them) give the player's soldiers and buildings new problems to answer, while piles and walls stay the heart of the game?** v4 answered its own question with a yes: soldiers and buildings work well together. Until now every enemy but the bloater has followed its route and hit only what was in its way, and a wall stopped everything short of a crush. v5 adds the **jumper**, which jumps a thin line, the **brute**, which breaks through one, and **perched** enemies, which an overflow or a jump lands on top of a building or the base. Spawning also gets **denser**, so the new enemies are tuned against the crowd the late waves bring. Piles and walls staying the heart of the game is the guardrail, as "never an RTS" was for v4.

It is self-contained: where v5 keeps a v4 rule, the rule is restated here, and `docs/specs/v4.md` stays frozen as history. Game rules and their starting numbers come from `docs/design.md`; vocabulary from `GLOSSARY.md`. If this spec and a closed ticket disagree, this spec is current. Planned in [Map: prototype v5 spec](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/221).

## 1. Platform and engine

Unchanged from v4.

- Godot 4.7.2, standard build, typed GDScript, pinned in one CI workflow variable.
- Platforms: macOS and Windows. Baseline machine: Apple M5 MacBook, 16 GB RAM.
- Performance target: about 1,000 enemies on screen at 60 fps on the baseline machine, measured on the **release** build (the official export template). Section 5 sets what counts as done.
- No C#. The enemies' neighbour scan runs in the C++ GDExtension `NeighbourScan` behind the `Enemies` module (ADR 0001), with the GDScript scan kept beside it for tests and for builds without the extension. Any other hot loop moves to C++ only if a re-measure says so.

## 2. Architecture

Sources: ADR 0001, ADR 0002, [Code organisation and system boundaries](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/11).

The v4 architecture stands: a node-free `Simulation` owns every system, their seeded RNGs and the tick driver; the view draws it and turns input into commands; nothing under `src/sim/` extends `Node` or `Resource` or references `src/view/`, and the `lint` job enforces it. Commands drain at step 0, so a seed plus a list of (tick, command) pairs replays a run exactly; while paused, every queued command drains at once, as step 0 of the next tick run early. Systems never signal each other; the tick driver passes data between them, or a system is handed another at construction, as `Enemies` is handed `Structures`. A restart builds a fresh `Simulation`. Every building is in one `Buildings` registry, and enemies attack walls, buildings and the base through the `Structures` facade. Buildings and soldiers draw their ids from one counter, `Ids`, and kill credit is the enemy's last hitter, carried on the death list. Soldiers live in their own `Soldiers` system, apart from enemies.

### What changes in v5

- **Enemy kinds.** `Enemies.Kind` gains `JUMPER` and `BRUTE` beside `NORMAL` and `BLOATER`. Per-kind numbers (HP, speed, bounty, mass, structure damage, size) are read through `Settings` by kind, as HP, speed and bounty already are, so the hot loops index small per-kind tables rather than branching.
- **Enemy states.** The `tumbling` flag becomes a per-enemy **state**: `WALKING`, `TUMBLING`, `CROUCHING`, `JUMPING` or `PERCHED`. `TUMBLING`, `JUMPING` and `PERCHED` are **lifted**: the enemy doesn't walk, collide, separate, roll or track progress, and others don't separate from it or push it. A `CROUCHING` jumper holds its ground like an attacker. Every state is in the spatial hash and can be shot.
- **Mass among enemies.** Separation between two enemies is weighted by their masses, in both the GDScript scan and `NeighbourScan`, so a brute shoulders through the crowd (section 7).
- **A new `Perches` system** owns which enemies are perched on which building or the base, each one's spot and drop-off cell, and the room left on each. Tumbles and jumps claim room through it when they are planned.
- **A new `Jumps` system** plans jumps, runs each jumper's crouch, flight and cooldown, and lands it by the tumble's rule. It shares the path planning and landing with `Overflow` (section 12).
- **Waves** spawn by a per-wave **spawn plan**: bursts on a stretch of the edge, scattered enemies between them, and a kind for every slot (section 8).
- **Routing** is unchanged: jumpers and brutes sample the direct field that already exists.

### Systems

New and changed systems; the rest are as in v4.

| System | Owns | Interface (main parts) |
|---|---|---|
| `Enemies` | As in v4, with `states` in place of `tumbling`, and kinds `JUMPER` and `BRUTE` | as in v4; `spawn(pos, hp, kind)` puts jumpers and brutes on the direct route; `touching` skips lifted enemies; `set_state(index, state)` |
| `Waves` | The spawn plan: each slot's kind and whether it is scattered, the bursts' stretches | `size(wave)`, `special_counts(wave) -> {bloaters, jumpers, brutes}`, `start(wave)`, `spawn()` (step 2) |
| `Overflow` | As in v4 | as in v4; a tumble's planning ends perched on the first building with room, or on the base; brutes are never put in a front rank |
| `Perches` | Per enemy: what it is perched on (a building id or the base), its spot and its drop-off cell. Per building and the base: perched count and room | `claim(cell) -> bool`, `release_claim(cell)`, `perch(index, cell, drop_cell)`, `step()` (perched attacks, at step 4), `release_destroyed(building_ids)`, `drop_cells(deaths)` (step 9), `count(building_id)`, `room(building_id)`, `on_base_count()` |
| `Jumps` | Each jumper's cooldown, and each crouch and jump in flight | `try_start(index) -> bool` (from `Enemies.move`), `step()` (step 4), `take_knock_offs()`, `fall_back(deaths)` (step 9), `jump(id)` for the view |
| `Piles` | As in v4 | `queue_bodies(deaths)` queues 2 levels for a brute's body |
| `Buildings` | As in v4 | grinders also hurt the enemies perched on them |

### Commands (new and changed)

- Debug: `SpawnEnemies(kind, count)` sends `count` enemies of `kind` (normal, bloater, jumper or brute) from the current wave's first spawn edge (or a random one in the build phase) at this wave's HP, as one burst. They count towards nothing in the wave and pay their bounty. It is how QA and the tests field a jumper or a brute without waiting for wave 8 or 12.

### Signals out to the view (end of tick)

v4's signals stand, and v5 changes or adds:

- `enemies_left_changed(left, specials)`: `specials` carries the counts of bloaters, jumpers and brutes in the next wave during the build phase, and is empty during a wave.
- `perched_bodies(drops)`: each perched enemy that died this tick, with its position on the roof and its drop-off cell, for the short fall.

Enemy states, perches, crouches and jumps in flight change every tick, so the view reads them read-only each frame, as it reads positions.

## 3. Tick order

The v4 order. Step 4 grows: jumps and perched attacks run in it after the crowd and the overflow move.

0. Drain commands (builds, trains, rally points, orders, targeting, assignments, thresholds, next wave, restart, settings, debug actions). A successful build recomputes power at once and pushes any soldiers off its footprint. A Train in the build phase trains at once.
1. `Piles` lands bodies queued last tick (a brute's 2 levels among them). On the tick after a wave end, `Piles.decay()` and `Routing.start_rebuild()` run next. The step-1 change list (pile level changes, walls broken or erased, buildings built or destroyed, HP bucket changes) goes to `Routing.update` (into its pending fields), `Overflow.update` and `UnitPaths.update`. Then `Routing.step()` settles the pending fields under its budget and swaps them in once they are settled, and the decay rebuild swaps in once it is due.
2. `Waves` spawns by the spawn plan: bursts, scattered enemies and each slot's kind. `Barracks.step` runs each working barracks' training timer during a wave.
3. Rebuild the spatial hash.
4. **Move enemies:**
   1. `Enemies.move`: walking enemies steer, separate by mass and collide; attackers hold their ground and damage their structure (a brute by its factor). A ready jumper that would start attacking a structure asks `Jumps.try_start` instead, and crouches if a jump is planned (section 12).
   2. `Overflow.step()`: gauges fill and leak, crushes launch tumbles, tumbles move and land, perched on a building or the base when their plan says so.
   3. `Jumps.step()`: crouches count down, jumps fly and land, cooldowns count down.
   4. `Perches.step()`: buildings destroyed this tick release their perched enemies; every perched enemy attacks what it stands on.
5. **Move units** (`UnitPaths.step()`, then `Soldiers.step`): settle path fields under their budget; knock off soldiers on wall cells crossed by tumbles and jumps this tick; walk soldiers; push soldiers and walking enemies apart by mass; space soldiers out; then find enemies jammed against soldiers, which start attacking them (or light their fuse, for a bloater), and apply this tick's attacks.
6. Buildings act, in build order: working towers fire, working grinders hurt every walking enemy touching them and every enemy perched on them.
7. **Units fire** (`Soldiers.fire`), in soldier order.
8. Drones fly and repair (`RepairYards.step()`).
9. Remove dead enemies, then `Blasts.resolve` the deaths (enemies, buildings, the base, piles and soldiers), then `Perches.release_destroyed` for buildings a blast destroyed, then remove dead soldiers, then `Combos.expire(tick)` and `RunState.pay` each death in order. Where each body lands is settled before it is queued: a perched enemy's drops off its building (`Perches.drop_cells`), else a tumble's or jump's falls back where it started (`Overflow.fall_back`, `Jumps.fall_back`), else it lands where it died. Bodies go to `Piles.queue_bodies`.
10. `Waves` checks for wave end; on wave end, decay becomes due at step 1 of the next tick, drones fly home, every living soldier recovers to full HP, every barracks' queue trains at once, and the phase becomes build phase.

Then the end-of-tick signals are emitted. Removal waits until step 9, so enemy and soldier ids and indices are stable for the whole tick. The step profiler reads step 4 as a whole; jumps and perches are a few dozen enemies at most, so they get no steps of their own.

## 5. Performance

Sources: [Performance: does v5 need a plan before the build?](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/233), [v4 performance plan](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/158).

v5 needs **no performance pass before the build**. It adds no routing, and at 1,000 dense there are about 3–7 ms of headroom.

### Where v4 stands

On the M5 with the release build, 1,000 dense meets its target: frame p99 9.4–9.6 ms in `bench_profile`, 13.3 ms in `bench_frame` with the 50-soldier army, against 16.7 ms. The neighbour scan runs in `NeighbourScan` (C++). Field settling is budgeted at `SETTLE_CELLS_PER_TICK` cells a tick across both fields, invalidation and reseeding included, with median 6 and p95 22 ticks to swap in at 1,000 dense with the army.

**How a frame is measured:** the wall-clock time from one bench `_process` to the next, split at `frame_pre_draw` and `frame_post_draw` into tick, process, render and the rest (`tools/frame_timer.gd`).

### Where v5 adds cost

- **Denser spawning** brings 1,000 enemies on screen sooner in a real wave: about 61 spawns a second at wave 20 against 10 before. The cost per enemy doesn't change, but the real-wave target is reached with more of the wave alive.
- **The brute's 2-level bodies** change pile levels more often, which re-settles the fields more often. The settling budget already caps it per tick; the cost shows as a longer swap wait, which the brute ticket reports.
- **Mass in the neighbour scan** adds one lookup and one multiply per pair, in C++.
- **Jumps and perches** are a few dozen enemies at a time (5% of a wave, up to 32 on the base and 8 a building), with work per enemy no larger than a tumble's.

### What counts as done

v4's three targets stay, with the same thresholds, now measured with jumpers and brutes mixed into `BenchCrowd`'s crowd at their wave shares, tumbles and jumps perching on its towers and the base, and the army in the field. All on the M5 with the release build:

| Case | Target |
|---|---|
| 1,000 dense (`tools/bench_frame.tscn`, `tools/bench_profile.tscn`) | frame p99 ≤ 16.7 ms, so **tick p99 ≤ about 12 ms** |
| 1,390 dense (the wave-20 crowd) | frame **mean** ≤ 16.7 ms; the p99 may go over |
| A real wave 20 (jump to wave 20 with the bench's eight towers and an endless base, 7,200 ticks) | no tick over 16.7 ms |

### Order

1. **The bench grows with each new enemy.** Each ticket that adds a new enemy behaviour adds it to `BenchCrowd` and `bench_sim` (section 18). That PR loses its relative comparison once; later tickets are compared against a base that already has the behaviour.
2. **Per-ticket checks:** every ticket that touches enemies, overflow, piles, buildings or the tick driver pastes `bench_profile` dense-1000, with and without the army, in its PR's Performance section. The brute ticket also reports the field swap wait (median and p95 ticks).
3. **The v5 performance re-measure** comes after the perching, brute and jumper tickets and before QA. It measures the three targets on the finished build, posts the tables, and files follow-up tickets only if a target is missed, as v4's re-measure did.

## 6. Map, structures and routing

Unchanged from v4, except where the brute and the jumper take the direct route and perched enemies attack from above.

- **The map** is `data/maps/v3-flared.json` (112×76 cells, about 3,400 clear, spawn edges N, E and W with ragged flared mouths, a 4×4 base at (53, 54), four small pockets beside the chokepoints). `:` marks the no-build margin, 6 cells deep along every spawn edge. `v1.json` and `v2-narrow.json` stay selectable from the debug panel. The map format keeps version 1.
- **Structures:** walls, buildings and the base, under one rule. An enemy attacks one only when it is in the way: its route crosses it within 2 steps and it is pressed against it (within `separation_radius + wall_reach` of a structure cell's square), or it is jammed against it (no progress for `jam_seconds` while pressed). Every attacker deals 2 HP/s, or 10 HP/s for a brute (section 11), and holds its ground. A wall has 15 HP and drops to a level-3 pile at 0; a destroyed building leaves nothing; the base has 200 HP and the run ends at 0. A bloater lights its fuse where a normal enemy would attack, and a ready jumper crouches to jump (section 12). An enemy killed at a structure dies in front of it.
- **Perched enemies** attack the building or base they stand on, whether or not it is in anyone's way (section 10).
- **Soldiers are not structures.** They are never in `Occupancy`, never part of a barrier and never in the flow fields.
- **Routing:** two integration fields (**sensible** and **direct**) with true shortest-path parents, octile steps with no corner cutting, finite costs for walls and buildings by their time to break, incremental updates under the per-tick settling budget that equal a full rebuild exactly once settled, a worker rebuild after decay, route rolls (5% pile, 3% wall or building), wave-tail push-through (turn-back angle 90°), and lifted enemies taking no part in routing. Barracks count as buildings.
- **Locked to the direct route:** a jumper or a brute spawns on the direct route and stays on it. It never rolls, is never sent back to the sensible route on passing an obstacle, and the wave-tail push-through skips it. Landing from a tumble or a jump, or dropping from a destroyed building, puts it back on the direct route; every other enemy goes back to the sensible route, as in v4. Its route changes only as the direct field re-settles. No new field is added: each enemy already samples the field of its own route.

## 7. Enemies

Sources: [The brute: how it breaks through](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/224), [The jumper: how it moves and jumps](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/225), [Starting numbers for jumpers, brutes and perching](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/238).

- **Storage:** packed arrays and a spatial hash (ADR 0001), as in v4, with a per-enemy state in place of the tumbling flag (section 2). HP grows ×1.05 a wave for every kind. `Enemies.damage(id, amount, by)` records the last hitter; towers, grinders and soldiers pass their id, a blast passes its bloater's credit and debug kills pass `NONE`. The death list holds each dead enemy's id, position, body position, kind, killer and blast flag, in removal order.
- **Movement:** separation, personal space, collision, jam detection, pace (±3%), wander and heading offset as in v4, one neighbour scan over the 3×3 buckets for both pushes. Each kind walks at its own speed: 2 cells/s, a bloater 1.6, a jumper 2.4, a brute 1.4, slowed by piles 15/30/45/60%.
- **Mass among enemies:** each enemy's separation push from a neighbour is scaled by `2 × m_other / (m_self + m_other)`. Two enemies of equal mass push as in v4, exactly. A brute (mass 3) beside a normal enemy (mass 1) takes half the push it would and gives one and a half times as much, so it shoulders through the crowd while the crowd still slows it. Personal space is unweighted. `NeighbourScan` takes a per-enemy mass array (filled from the kinds at spawn) and the GDScript scan matches it; the scan comparison test covers mixed masses.
- **Soldier contact by mass:** in the soldier's contact pass, the enemy takes `m_soldier / (m_soldier + m_enemy)` of the overlap and the soldier the rest: 5/6 for a mass-1 enemy as in v4, 5/8 for a brute, so a brute pushes a soldier line further than an enemy does.
- **Soldier targets** as in v4: an enemy attacking a soldier holds its ground like an attacker at step 4 and doesn't count as stalled. Only walking enemies take a soldier target. A perched enemy never attacks a soldier.
- **Targeting** is unchanged: towers and soldiers keep the two-way toggle, `bloaters_first` (the default) or `closest_to_base`, through `Enemies.target_in_range`, which ranks by the sensible field's value at the enemy's position. Every state can be targeted. A perched enemy stands on a building cell, whose sensible value is lower than the crowd it crossed, so it ranks ahead of that crowd, and one on the base (value 0) ranks ahead of everything; under `bloaters_first` a bloater in range still wins. A jumper in the air is ranked by where it is, as a tumbling enemy is. There is no option for perched enemies, jumpers or brutes.
- **The bloater:** unchanged. 30 HP, 1.6 cells/s, bounty 2, drawn ×1.5 in its own colour, colliding at the normal radius, no body. Where a normal enemy would start attacking a structure, or a soldier, it lights a 1 s fuse and holds its ground without damage; when the fuse runs out it dies with no killer. Killing it during the fuse blows it up there, credited to its killer. Starting a tumble puts its fuse out; perching lights it again at once (section 10).

## 8. Spawning and waves

Sources: [Jumpers and brutes in the waves](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/226), [Starting numbers for jumpers, brutes and perching](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/238).

### Wave size and special enemies

- **Wave size** is unchanged: `round(20 × 1.25^(n−1))`, about 149 at wave 10, 455 at 15 and 1,390 at 20. One or two of the map's spawn edges, as in v4.
- **Bloaters** from wave 5, unchanged: a base count of `1 + (wave − 5) ÷ 6` (rounded down), and a roll fixed per run and wave for one fewer (25%) or one more (10%).
- **Jumpers** from wave 8: `round(size × 5%)` (about 5 at wave 8, 23 at 15, 70 at 20), capped at `jumpers.cap` when it is above 0. The cap starts at 0, off.
- **Brutes** from wave 12, by the bloater's model with their own settings: a base of `1 + (wave − 12) ÷ 6`, rolled per run and wave for one fewer (25%) or one more (10%). The roll is seeded apart from the bloater's (`hash([seed, "brutes", wave])`), so the two never move together.
- **Placement:** each special enemy takes a random slot of the wave's spawn plan (bursts and scattered alike), drawn without replacement, bloaters first, then jumpers, then brutes, taking a normal enemy's place, so the wave size is unchanged. If they would outnumber the wave, the later kinds get fewer. There are no groups and no brutes leading.
- **Enemies left** = wave size − kills this wave. In the build phase it shows the next wave's size and its special counts (`Waves.special_counts`), which match the wave exactly.

### Bursts and scattered enemies

- **The spawn plan** is built at wave start. A wave of size N has `S = round(N × scatter_share)` **scattered** slots and `N − S` slots in **bursts** of `b = round(burst_size × burst_growth^(n−1))` (16, about 25 at wave 10, 40 at wave 20), the last one short. The scattered slots are spread evenly between the bursts: the k-th scattered enemy (k from 0) comes after burst `floor(k × bursts ÷ S)`.
- **The rate:** spawning runs at `spawn_rate × spawn_rate_growth^(n−1)` enemies a second (10, about 24 at wave 10, 61 at wave 20). Each tick adds the rate to a budget; a burst spawns when the budget covers its size, and a scattered enemy when it covers one, so the average rate holds. A burst lands every 1.6 s at wave 1, about 1.0 s at wave 10 and 0.66 s at wave 20.
- **A burst's stretch:** each burst takes its edge in turn across the wave's edges, as in v4, picks a random spawn cell of that edge as its centre, and spawns on the edge's spawn cells within half the stretch's width of it, measured along the edge. The width is `burst_width × sqrt(b ÷ burst_size)`, at least 1 cell: 6 cells for a burst of 16, about 9.5 for 40. Each enemy takes a random cell of the stretch, with v4's jitter (±0.25 cells).
- **A scattered enemy** spawns alone on a random spawn cell of a random one of the wave's edges, with the same jitter.
- **The wave tail** is unchanged: Enemies left ≤ 10 + 2 × the wave number. **Wave end:** every enemy spawned and none alive, perched ones included. Soldiers don't count towards any of it.

## 9. Piles, overflow and blasts

Unchanged from v4 except for the brute's body, perching and the low fall.

- **Piles and walls:** five levels (15/30/45/60% slow, then a 15 HP wall); bodies land on the cell their enemy died on or the nearest valid cell from the death position; a broken wall drops to a level-3 pile; decay is off (both chances 0); `Piles.erase(cell)` clears a cell, and only a blast erases. A dead soldier leaves a body by the same rule.
- **A brute's body** adds 2 levels to the cell it lands on, stopping at a wall: on a level-3 pile it makes a wall, and on a level-4 pile it makes a wall and the second level is lost. It lands by the normal rule, so a cell that is already a wall sends it to the nearest valid cell. Killed by a blast, it leaves no body.
- **Where a body lands**, in order: a perched enemy's drops off its building to its drop-off cell (section 10); a tumble's or a jump's, killed in the air or within 1 s of landing, falls back to where it started; anything else lands where it died.
- **Overflow:** barriers (connected wall and building cells, 8-way) relabelled on change, the 3-cell band, progress and stalled enemies, the gauge (fill 1.5 per stalled enemy per second, leak 40, clamp 0–120, active above 10 until below 4), tiers (trickle 20, stream 50, surge 90) launching 0.5, 3 or 15 tumbles a second from the front rank with 2 relief each. Attackers tumble only when every front enemy is attacking. A tumble moves at 180% of its kind's walking speed, raised and shootable, ignoring collision, slow and soldiers, ground by each working grinder cell it enters, recording each wall cell it crosses for the knock-off, and landing back on its route.
- **Tumble planning** is v4's, straight over the touched barrier cell facing the base, else along the direct field's parent chain, now with perching. Walking the planned path cell by cell:
  - a **building** cell whose building has room (claimed through `Perches.claim`) ends the tumble there, **perched**; a full building is crossed;
  - a **wall** cell is crossed; walls never hold a perched enemy;
  - the **base** ends it perched on the base if the base has room, else on the free cell beside the base nearest where it reached it, as in v4 (the enemy then attacks the base from the ground); with no free cell either, the tumble isn't planned;
  - else the first clear or pile cell with fewer than 6 enemies ends it on the ground, as in v4.
- **Who is never picked to tumble:** a brute; a crouching or jumping jumper; any lifted enemy. A stalled brute still counts as one enemy towards its crush and its gauge. A crouching jumper is not stalled.
- **Blasts** resolve at the removal step, breadth-first through chains: every living enemy within 2 cells of a blast's centre dies as a blast kill credited to the bloater's killer and leaves no body, perched, tumbling and jumping enemies included; every building with a cell centre within 2 cells takes 30 once per blast, as does the base; every pile and wall with its cell centre within 2 cells is erased; every living soldier within 2 cells takes 30 once per blast. The edge is hard. A blast that destroys a building releases its perched enemies at step 9 (section 10).

## 10. Perched enemies

Sources: [Enemies on top of buildings](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/223), [The jumper: how it moves and jumps](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/225), [Starting numbers for jumpers, brutes and perching](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/238).

- **Perched:** an enemy standing on top of a building or the base, where a tumble or a jump landed it. Its state is `PERCHED`.
- **Room:** a building holds `perch_room` (2) perched enemies per footprint cell: 8 on a 2×2 building, 2 on a pylon. The base holds `base_perch_room` (2) per cell, 32 on its 4×4. Room is claimed when a tumble or jump is planned, so tumbles and jumps in flight never overfill it, and the claim is given back if the enemy dies in the air. A setting lowered below the perched count lets no more on and drops no one.
- **The spot:** each footprint cell has `perch_room` spots spread across it. A perched enemy takes the free spot nearest where its path entered the building, its position moves there, and it stays there. Spots are a constant layout in code; the count per cell comes from the setting.
- **The drop-off cell** is set when it perches: walking its path back from the building, the first cell that is not a wall, a building or the base, else the cell it started from. Bodies so drop on the side the enemy came from, never on a building and never across a wall.
- **Up there (step 4, `Perches.step`):** every perched enemy deals `structure_damage` (2 HP/s) to what it stands on, whatever its state: unpowered and under repair change nothing. A brute never perches, so the factor never applies here. It never walks along a barrier, takes no soldier target, is never part of a crush and fills no gauge. It is in the spatial hash, so towers and soldiers shoot it, but walking enemies and soldiers don't separate from it or push it.
- **A perched bloater** lights its fuse at once on perching and holds without damage; when it blows, its blast hits the building under it, the base or its neighbours as any blast does.
- **Grinders:** a working grinder hurts every enemy perched on it at its usual 3 HP/s at step 6, with kill credit and its combo. An enemy perched on a neighbouring building isn't touching it: `Enemies.touching` skips lifted enemies, and the grinder adds its own perched enemies from `Perches`.
- **Getting down:** only when its building is destroyed, by attack at step 4 or by a blast at step 9. Its perched enemies become `WALKING` where they stand on the freed footprint, with their jam and progress reset, on the sensible route (the direct route for a jumper), and walk from the next step 4. Enemies perched on the base never get down: the run ends when it falls.
- **Death:** a perched enemy killed by any cause but a blast leaves its body at its drop-off cell, and `perched_bodies` reports it for the short fall. A blast kill leaves none.
- **Details:** `Perches.count(id)` and `Perches.room(id)` feed the selected building's or the base's **Perched: n / max**.

## 11. The brute

Sources: [The brute: how it breaks through](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/224), [Reading the new enemies](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/237), [Starting numbers for jumpers, brutes and perching](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/238).

- **Stats:** 40 HP (growing ×1.05 a wave), 1.4 cells/s, bounty 4, mass 3. Drawn ×1.8 a normal enemy's size in steel grey-blue, colliding at the normal radius.
- **Route:** the direct route, locked (section 6).
- **Breaking:** it attacks a structure in its way by the one structure rule, dealing `structure_damage × structure_damage_factor` (10 HP/s): a wall in 1.5 s, a tower in 6 s, the base in 20 s. A wall it destroys drops to level 3. Piles at levels 1–4 slow it like any enemy; it never breaks or flattens them.
- **Body:** 2 levels, stopping at a wall (section 9).
- **Crowd:** mass 3 in separation and soldier contact (section 7). Never picked to tumble, so it never perches. Counts as one stalled enemy towards a crush.
- **Soldiers:** it attacks a soldier only by the in-its-way rule, at 2 HP/s.
- **Answers:** towers, grinders and soldiers hurt it like any enemy; a blast kills it outright.

## 12. The jumper

Sources: [The jumper: how it moves and jumps](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/225), [Feel: jumps and tumbles onto buildings](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/236), [Reading the new enemies](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/237), [Starting numbers for jumpers, brutes and perching](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/238).

- **Stats:** a normal enemy's HP (10, growing ×1.05 a wave), bounty 1 and mass 1, at 2.4 cells/s. Drawn at a normal enemy's size in purple.
- **Route:** the direct route, locked (section 6).

### When it jumps

- **Ready:** a walking jumper with no cooldown left.
- **The trigger:** in `Enemies.move`, at the moment a ready jumper would take a structure target by the in-the-way rule (and before it deals any damage), it calls `Jumps.try_start`. If a jump is planned, it takes no structure target and starts to crouch; if not, it attacks as a normal enemy that tick and tries again on each later tick it would attack.
- **Planning** follows the tumble's straight plan, then the direct field's parent chain, from the jumper's position, counting the wall and building cells crossed:
  - a **building** cell with room ends the jump perched there (room claimed);
  - a wall or full building cell is crossed, and counts towards the reach;
  - more than `reach` (3) wall and building cells before a landing means **too thick**: no jump;
  - the **base** ends it perched on the base if it has room; a full base means no jump, and the jumper attacks the base from the ground. A jumper never jumps over the base;
  - the first clear or pile cell with fewer than 6 enemies after at least one structure cell ends it on the ground. Rock or the map's edge first means no jump.
- **Can't jump** (too thick, a full base, no landing, or on cooldown): it acts as a normal enemy. It attacks, counts as stalled towards a crush and its gauge, and can be picked to tumble. A tumble isn't its jump and doesn't start its cooldown.
- **Piles:** it never jumps them; levels 1–4 slow it like any enemy.

### The jump

- **Crouch:** `CROUCHING` for `crouch` (0.25 s, 15 ticks), holding its ground like an attacker, dealing no damage, not stalled and never picked to tumble. It can be shot. A bloater's fuse doesn't apply: a jumper is never a bloater.
- **In the air:** `JUMPING` for `air_time` (0.8 s, 48 ticks) whatever the jump's length, moving along its planned path at the constant speed that covers it in that time. It ignores collision with the obstacle, the crowd and soldiers, is ground by each working grinder cell it enters as a tumble is, and records each wall cell it crosses for the knock-off (`Jumps.take_knock_offs`, merged with Overflow's at step 5). It can be shot and killed.
- **Landing:** perched, or on the ground back on the direct route. A jumper that lands against a soldier attacks it by the in-its-way rule. Its **cooldown** of 4 s starts on landing and counts down whatever it does, perched or not.
- **Fall-back:** killed in the air or within 1 s of landing on the ground, its body falls back to where it crouched.
- **Pressure:** a jump takes nothing off any gauge and is never part of an overflow.
- **Changes mid-jump:** the path is fixed when the crouch starts. A building on it destroyed before the jumper perches there gives the claim back, and the jumper lands on the freed cell on the ground.

## 13. Buildings, power, repair, towers and grinders

Unchanged from v4, with perched enemies on them.

| Kind | Footprint | Cost | HP | Power area | Perch room | Hotkey |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tower | 2×2 | 120 | 60 | 4 | 8 | T |
| Grinder | 2×2 | 90 | 100 | 2 | 8 | G |
| Pylon | 1×1 | 30 | 20 | 10 | 2 | P |
| Repair yard | 2×2 | 120 | 60 | 4 | 8 | R |
| Barracks | 2×2 | 200 | 100 | 3 | 8 | B |
| Base (not a building) | 4×4 | – | 200 | 10 | 32 | – |

- **Power:** square power areas from the footprint's edge (Chebyshev), powered through overlaps outwards from the always-powered base, recomputed whenever the set of buildings changes. An unpowered building stops working but stays a structure.
- **Working:** powered, and not under repair unless it is a pylon. `Buildings.is_working(id)` is the one check towers, grinders, drones and barracks read.
- **Placement (step 0):** every footprint cell in bounds, not no-build, on the power grid and clear (no rock, base, building or pile), then the barracks limit for a barracks, then gold. Rejections: "off the map", "too close to a spawn edge", "off the power grid", "occupied", "barracks limit reached" or "not enough gold". A building placed over soldiers pushes them off. Building is allowed during waves, in the build phase and while paused. No selling or demolishing.
- **Repair yards:** one drone each, straight flight at 8 cells/s, 5 HP/s until full, repair area 8, a threshold of 25/50/75/100% (50% when built), one assignment, auto picking nearest the yard then lowest HP then lowest id, preferring a building no other drone targets. Drones repair buildings only, never soldiers, walls or the base, and only during waves. A drone repairs a building with enemies perched on it like any other.
- **Towers:** 5 damage, 3.5 shots/s, range 8 from the footprint's centre, instant hits, firing in build order, cooldown in ticks, ready when built, targeting per tower (section 7). A tower shoots the enemies perched on it like any other.
- **Grinders:** 3 HP/s to every walking enemy touching them (within `separation_radius + wall_reach` of a cell's square) and to every enemy perched on them, 3 at once per grinder cell a tumble or jump enters, kill credit and their own combo. Grinders never hurt soldiers.

## 14. Soldiers

Unchanged from v4 except where they meet the new enemies. `docs/specs/v4.md` sections 9–14 hold the full record of how v4 built them; the rules are these.

- **Barracks and training:** a 2×2 building under every building rule (200 gold, 100 HP, power area 3), at most 5 at once. Capacity is 10 per barracks standing; used is living plus queued soldiers. `Train(barracks_id, count)` queues 1 or 5 soldiers at 30 gold each, as many as the queue (5), capacity and gold allow; `CancelTraining` refunds. Training is instant in the build phase and takes 5 s during a wave while the barracks is working; every queue trains at once at wave end; a destroyed barracks refunds its queue. A new soldier appears on the free ring cell nearest the base, else the nearest free cell outwards. A rally point gives each new soldier a Move to it in Cluster.
- **Soldiers:** packed arrays in `Soldiers`, ids from `Ids`. A standing soldier holds its spot and walks back when pushed off it; a soldier under an order walks its own path at 2.5 cells/s, slowed by piles and 75% on a wall. Buildings, rock, the base and the map's edge are solid to soldiers; walls and piles are not.
- **Contact:** at step 5, soldiers and walking enemies push apart by mass (section 7). Soldiers keep a soft personal space of 0.7 cells from each other. Lifted enemies (tumbling, jumping, perched) are never pushed.
- **Orders:** `Move(ids, target, formation)` in Cluster or Line, `Stop(ids)`, per-order path fields on `UnitPaths` under `PATH_SETTLE_CELLS` a tick, never the enemies' flow fields. Selection, control groups and the Formation setting are the view's.
- **Fighting:** 2 damage at 2 shots/s, range 4 plus 0.5 a level of elevation, by the tower's targeting rules, firing standing or walking (at half speed while it has a target). Kills pay bounty × the soldier's own combo tier. Soldiers shoot perched, tumbling and jumping enemies like any other.
- **Enemies fight back:** a walking enemy pressed against a soldier and jammed attacks it at 2 HP/s, a brute included, unless the soldier stands on a wall (elevation 5). A bloater lights its fuse instead. Blasts deal 30 to every soldier within 2 cells. Grinders never hurt soldiers. Perched enemies never attack soldiers, and soldiers can't push them off.
- **Elevation and knock-off:** elevation is the pile level under the soldier. A tumble **or a jump** that crosses the wall cell a soldier stands on knocks it off to the nearest free ground cell to that tumble's or jump's landing cell, never back across the wall; with no ground on that side, it lands on the landing cell itself. For a tumble or jump that perches, the landing cell is its drop-off cell (section 10), so the soldier lands on the side of the building the enemy came from, never on a roof.
- **Death and recovery:** a soldier at 0 HP is removed at step 9, freeing capacity and losing its combo, and leaves one body unless a blast killed it. Every living soldier recovers to full HP at wave end.

## 15. Run state and combos

Unchanged from v4.

- **Run state:** base HP 200 and gold 300 at the start; at 0 base HP the run ends, `game_over` fires and only a restart is accepted. Kills counts every enemy death in the run.
- **Bounty:** each kind's bounty (1, a bloater 2, a jumper 1, a brute 4) × its killer's tier. A bloater that blows itself up pays nothing of its own, and its blast kills pay ×1.
- **Combos** per killer (building or soldier): count, tier, a 2 s timer and best this run. A credited kill adds 1, sets the tier (×1 below 10, ×2 from 10, ×3 from 25, cap ×3) and restarts the timer; a timer that runs out breaks the combo at step 9, before paying. A death with no killer, or whose killer was destroyed or died this tick, pays ×1 and feeds no combo. A building or soldier that kills a bloater gets every kill its blast makes.

## 16. View

The v4 view stands: plain coloured shapes from one committed atlas, one y-sorted world, pooled enemy and soldier canvas items, pile lift, hit flash, tracers, drones, zoom, UI scaling, the bottom HUD, selection and orders, combo rings, pressure gauges, blast rings, the merged range shape and rally flags. v5 adds:

### Drawing

- **Kinds:** the enemy sprite is shared; each kind is told apart by colour and size, as the bloater is. A jumper is purple at a normal enemy's size and keeps it through the crouch and the jump; like any enemy it is tinted orange only while tumbling. A brute is steel grey-blue at ×1.8 (`brutes.size`), never orange since it never tumbles. Neither has an animation of its own. These looks are stand-ins until the final art.
- **The tumble** is drawn as a low fall: rising just over the top of what it crosses (the tallest wall, building or pile on its path), then dropping beyond, still tinted orange. It replaces v3's flat raised slide.
- **The jump:** during the crouch, the jumper squashes down on the spot. In the air it follows an arc whose lift runs from where it starts to where it lands (a roof when it perches), peaking `jump_clearance` (24 px) above the tallest wall, building, base or pile on its path. It never rises a fixed height. The view reads the jump's path cells from `Jumps.jump(id)` and works out the heights itself.
- **Perched:** drawn standing on its roof, raised to its building's or the base's height, with no marker or attack bob.
- **Perched bodies:** a short fall from the roof to the drop-off cell where `perched_bodies` reports one, pooled like the soldier death effect. The body lands on its pile at once in the sim.
- **Heights** of buildings and the base for the arc, the low fall and the perch come from the atlas sprites (the sprite's height less its footprint's diamond), so they follow the art.

### HUD

- **Enemies left:** in the build phase, the next wave's size followed by each special type with a count above zero, in the order bloaters, jumpers, brutes ("120 enemies · 3 bloaters · 6 jumpers · 1 brute"). During a wave it stays one number. Nothing announces a type's first wave.
- **Building and base details** add **Perched: n / max**.

## 18. Testing, CI and performance

Sources: [Testing, CI and performance checks](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/10), [Performance: does v5 need a plan before the build?](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/233).

### The test seam

- **The Simulation is the seam**, as in v4: build a `Simulation` on a tiny inline map (`TestMaps.from_rows`) with fixed settings and seed, queue commands (`SpawnEnemies` among them), run ticks, and assert on public state and end-of-tick signals.
- New public state to assert on: each enemy's kind, state and route; the spawn plan (burst sizes, stretches, scattered slots, each slot's kind) and where enemies appeared; special counts per wave; separation pushes between masses; a brute's damage to structures and its 2-level body; tumble and jump paths and landings; who is perched where, room and claims, drop-off cells and where perched bodies land; perched enemies released by a destroyed building; jump cooldowns; knock-offs from jumps.
- **Kept seams:** the routing exactness test stays at `Routing`; the scan comparison test checks `NeighbourScan` against the GDScript scan, now with mixed masses; `bench_sim` and the local benchmarks stay the performance seam.
- Each build ticket's acceptance criteria name the tests it adds, written test-first. Rendering, input and UI (colours, sizes, the arc, the low fall, the roof, the short fall, Enemies left, Perched n / max) have no automated tests; the human checks them in playtests.

### The bench grows with the enemies

- **Denser spawning** needs no bench change: `BenchCrowd` tops its crowd up itself, and the real wave 20 spawns by the new plan.
- **Brutes:** `BenchCrowd` mixes brutes into the crowd it tops up at their wave-20 count, on the direct route, breaking through its ring of towers and the walls it raises. `bench_sim` checks that a brute damaged a structure.
- **Perching:** `BenchCrowd` keeps a crush against its towers that overflows, so tumbles perch on the towers and the base. `bench_sim` checks that enemies perched.
- **Jumpers:** `BenchCrowd` mixes jumpers into its crowd at 5% of what it tops up, jumping its walls and towers. `bench_sim` checks that jumps happened.
- The PR that grows the bench loses its relative comparison that one time, and says so.

### CI

CI keeps `lint`, `test`, `sim-budget` and `export`, all required on every PR. `sim-budget` stays at 1.2× the base's best tick mean on the same runner, with bench_sim's 80 ms backstop, and its limit changes only in a PR that explains why. The native library is rebuilt by the existing workflow when `native/` changes.

## 19. QA and tuning

Not build tickets; done in the last ticket, worked with the human:

- The human plays the finished build and tunes the v5 numbers live in the debug panel. The agent records each session's settings (from `data/settings/tuning-sessions/`) and the human's notes in the ticket, and suggests improvements between sessions. Kept values go into `defaults.json` and `docs/design.md`, and v5's result into `docs/design.md`'s Prototype results.
- **v5's question:** do enemies that beat walls in new ways give the player's soldiers and buildings new problems to answer, while piles and walls stay the heart of the game?
- **Playtest questions:**
  - Do piles and walls stay the heart of the game, or do jumpers and brutes make walls feel pointless? Thickness and layers should answer jumpers; a brute's breach should half-fill with its own body.
  - Are too many enemies perched on buildings? The jumper cap (`jumpers.cap`) and perch room are the levers.
  - Does a base overrun from above feel fair, with towers and soldiers in range shooting enemies off it?
  - The jumper's reach, cooldown and speed, and the brute's HP, speed, mass, bounty and factor, by feel.
  - Does the denser spawning read as bursts early and one flood late, and does it bring the late waves the menace the human wanted?
  - Do soldiers have a new job (answering perched enemies and jumpers inside the defence), and do buildings (thick blocks, grinders under a crush)?
- **The performance tables:** the M5 release tables from section 5's targets, on the finished build.
