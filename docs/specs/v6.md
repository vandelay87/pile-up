# Prototype v6 spec: mining

This spec gathers every technical decision for prototype v6 in one place and ends in the ordered list of build tickets. v6 asks one question: **does mining as the only income, with the player choosing how hard to mine (and so how many enemies come), make a richer risk-and-reward game than waves and bounty?** v5 answered its own question with a yes: enemies that beat walls in new ways gave soldiers and buildings new problems, and piles and walls stayed the heart of the game. Until now the player pressed Next wave, waves grew by number, and kills paid bounty times a combo. In v6 the base is a drill. It mines its **deposit** at a **pace** the player sets (Off, Low, Medium or High). Mining is the only income, and it fills the **alert**: a wave comes when the alert is full, sized by **threat**, which rises with time and with every mineral mined. **Rigs** mine the deposits further out and draw enemies to themselves. A run is won by **extraction** once a **quota** of Aether is mined, and lost when the base falls. Piles and walls staying the heart of the game is the guardrail, as "never an RTS" was for v4.

It is self-contained: where v6 keeps a v5 rule, the rule is restated here, and `docs/specs/v5.md` stays frozen as history. Game rules and their starting numbers come from `docs/design.md`; vocabulary from `GLOSSARY.md`. If this spec and a closed ticket disagree, this spec is current. Planned in [Map: prototype v6 spec](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/265).

## 1. Platform and engine

Unchanged from v5.

- Godot 4.7.2, standard build, typed GDScript, pinned in one CI workflow variable.
- Platforms: macOS and Windows. Baseline machine: Apple M5 MacBook, 16 GB RAM. Windows is the main platform, but its performance waits for its own wayfinding session after the prototype (section 5).
- Performance target: about 1,000 enemies on screen at 60 fps on the baseline machine, measured on the **release** build (the official export template). Section 5 sets what counts as done. Waves have no cap, and past about 1,000 there is no 60 fps promise.
- No C#. The enemies' neighbour scan runs in the C++ GDExtension `NeighbourScan` behind the `Enemies` module (ADR 0001), with the GDScript scan kept beside it for tests and for builds without the extension. Any other hot loop moves to C++ only if a re-measure says so.

## 2. Architecture

Sources: ADR 0001, ADR 0002, [Code organisation and system boundaries](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/11).

The v5 architecture stands: a node-free `Simulation` owns every system, their seeded RNGs and the tick driver; the view draws it and turns input into commands; nothing under `src/sim/` extends `Node` or `Resource` or references `src/view/`, and the `lint` job enforces it. Commands drain at step 0, so a seed plus a list of (tick, command) pairs replays a run exactly; while paused, every queued command drains at once, as step 0 of the next tick run early. Systems never signal each other; the tick driver passes data between them, or a system is handed another at construction. A restart builds a fresh `Simulation`. Every building is in one `Buildings` registry, and enemies attack walls, buildings and the base through the `Structures` facade. Buildings and soldiers draw their ids from one counter, `Ids`. Enemy states, perches, jumps and tumbles are as in v5.

### What changes in v6

- **Mining replaces bounty.** A new `Miners` system owns the base and every rig as **miners**: each one's deposit, what is left of it, its mix and whether it is mining or a target. It mines each tick at the pace and pays gold into `RunState`.
- **Threat replaces the wave number.** A new `Threat` system holds the run's threat, raised by time and by every mineral mined. Every rule that read the wave number reads threat.
- **The alert replaces Next wave and the build phase.** `Waves` owns the alert, the open waves (one at a time, several only in the extraction siege), each wave's spawn plan and the scattered **trickle**. Its phases are `QUIET` and `WAVE`; `BUILD` is retired.
- **Each enemy knows its wave.** `Enemies` gains a per-enemy `wave` index: the wave it belongs to, or `-1` for the trickle and debug spawns. Enemies left and a wave's end count per wave.
- **Routing has several seeds.** The fields are seeded from every target miner (the base, and each running rig), so an enemy heads for whichever is nearest by route cost. A change of seeds rebuilds both fields on the worker threads.
- **Readers that meant "the base" mean "the miner this route ends at"** where a rig can stand in for it: tumble and jump planning along the direct parents, and a jumper never jumping over one (section 11). Perches and blasts already treat a rig as a building.
- **A new `Extraction` system** runs the quota, the Extract command and the siege's countdown, and sets the run's outcome.
- **The combo goes.** `Combos`, `RunState.pay`, every bounty setting and the `combo` group are removed. Kill credit stays on the death list (blasts still credit their bloater's killer), though nothing pays on it.
- **Repair yards repair soldiers** and work whenever powered (section 12).

### Systems

New and changed systems; the rest are as in v5.

| System | Owns | Interface (main parts) |
|---|---|---|
| `Miners` | The pace. Per miner (the base, and each rig by building id): its deposit's starting contents and what is left per mineral, its switch (rigs), and its seed cells | `set_pace(pace)`, `set_switch(rig_id, on)`, `step(extracting) -> MinedThisTick` (step 2), `is_mining(id)`, `is_target(id)`, `is_dry(id)`, `all_dry() -> bool`, `mining_rigs() -> int`, `remaining(id) -> {gold, aether}`, `target_seeds() -> Dictionary[Vector2i, int]`, `take_seed_change() -> bool`, `target_of(cell) -> int`, `gold_rate() -> float` |

| `Waves` | The alert and its phase, the edges picked for the next wave, each open wave's size, plan, edges and enemies left, the trickle's budget | `step(pace, threat, mining_rigs, dry, siege)` (step 2: fill, start, spawn), `size_for(threat, pace_factor, rigs, index)`, `special_counts(threat, size, index)`, `record_kills(deaths)`, `check_end() -> bool` (step 10), `in_wave_tail(wave)`, `enemies_left`, `alert`, `phase` |
| `Extraction` | Its state (`LOCKED`, `READY`, `RUNNING`, `DONE`) and the countdown in ticks | `check_quota(aether)`, `start() -> bool`, `step()` (step 2), `is_siege()`, `ticks_left` |

| `Combos` | Removed | |

### Commands (new and changed)

- `SetPace(pace)`: Off, Low, Medium or High. Takes effect at once. Rejected once every miner is dry ("every deposit is dry") or during the siege ("extracting").
- `SetRigSwitch(rig_id, on)`: switches a rig on or off, at any time, with no cooldown.
- `Extract`: starts extraction. Rejected before the quota is met ("quota not met") or once started.
- `Assign(yard_id, target_id)`: the target may now be a soldier.
- `Build(kind, origin)`: gains the `rig` kind (section 9).
- **Removed:** `NextWave` as a player command.
- **Debug:** `JumpToThreat(threat)` replaces `JumpToWave`. `SendWave` fills the alert at once, so the next wave starts this tick (and picks its edges if it hadn't). `AddAether(amount)` adds Aether as if mined, without threat. `StrikeVein(miner_id)` strikes a rich vein (section 7). `SpawnEnemies(kind, count)` is as in v5: the enemies belong to no wave (`wave` = `-1`), from the next wave's edges if they are picked, else a random edge.

### Signals out to the view (end of tick)

v5's signals stand, except as below.

- **Removed:** `combo_events`, `payout`, `game_over`.
- **Changed:** `enemies_left_changed(left, size)`: the open waves' enemies left and their total size, sent only during a wave. `phase_changed(phase)` carries `QUIET` or `WAVE`. `wave_started(edges, size, specials)` adds the wave's size and its special counts for the announcement.
- **New:** `wave_coming(edges)` at 75% alert; `wave_ended`; `pace_changed(pace, locked)`; `miner_changed(id)` (built, switched, powered or unpowered, ran dry); `wave_targets(miner_ids)`, the miners the coming or open wave is heading for (section 9); `vein_struck(miner_id, gold)`; `quota_met`; `extraction_started`; `run_ended(outcome)`.

Threat, the alert's fill, the mining rate, the Aether mined, what is left in each deposit and the countdown change every tick, so the view reads them read-only each frame, as it reads positions.

## 3. Tick order

The v5 order. Step 2 grows: mining, threat, the alert and extraction run there before spawning. Step 9 no longer pays.

0. Drain commands (builds, pace, rig switches, Extract, trains, rally points, orders, targeting, assignments, thresholds, restart, settings, debug actions). A successful build recomputes power at once and pushes any soldiers off its footprint.
1. `Piles` lands bodies queued last tick (a brute's 2 levels among them). If `Miners.take_seed_change()` reports that the set of target miners changed since last tick (a rig built, destroyed, switched, or losing or gaining power), `Routing.set_seeds` starts a rebuild (section 6). On the tick after a wave end, `Piles.decay()` runs and joins the same rebuild. A rebuild swaps in 12 ticks after it started. The step-1 change list (pile level changes, walls broken or erased, buildings built or destroyed, HP bucket changes) goes to `Routing.update`, `Overflow.update` and `UnitPaths.update`. Then `Routing.step()` settles the pending fields under its budget and swaps them in once they are settled.
2. **Mine and spawn:**
   1. `Miners.step()` mines each mining miner's share of its deposit and rolls for rich veins; `RunState.credit` adds the gold and the minerals mined; `Threat.add_minerals` adds every mineral; `Extraction.check_quota` unlocks Extract once the Aether mined reaches the quota.
   2. `Threat.step()` adds the time.
   3. `Extraction.step()` counts the siege down.
   4. `Waves.step()`: the alert fills, picks the next wave's edges at 75% and starts a wave when full; every open wave spawns by its plan; the trickle spawns.
   5. `Barracks.step` runs each working barracks' training timer, at any time.
3. Rebuild the spatial hash.
4. **Move enemies** (as in v5): `Enemies.move`, `Overflow.step()`, `Jumps.step()`, `Perches.step()`.
5. **Move units** (`UnitPaths.step()`, then `Soldiers.step`), as in v5.
6. Buildings act, in build order: working towers fire, working grinders hurt every walking enemy touching them and every enemy perched on them.
7. **Units fire** (`Soldiers.fire`), in soldier order. A soldier under repair holds fire.
8. Drones fly and repair buildings and soldiers (`RepairYards.step()`), whenever their yard is powered.
9. Remove dead enemies, then `Blasts.resolve` the deaths (enemies, buildings and rigs, the base, piles and soldiers), then `Perches.release_destroyed` for buildings a blast destroyed, then remove dead soldiers, then `RunState.record_kills` and `Waves.record_kills`. Where each body lands is settled as in v5, and bodies go to `Piles.queue_bodies`.
10. `Waves.check_end()`: a wave whose enemies have all spawned and died ends; once no wave enemies are left, the wave-end rules fire (decay becomes due at step 1 of the next tick) and the alert starts refilling. Then the run end: base HP 0 ends it as **Base destroyed**; else a siege countdown at 0 ends it as **Extracted**.

Then the end-of-tick signals are emitted. Removal waits until step 9, so enemy and soldier ids and indices are stable for the whole tick. The step profiler gains no step: step 2 is read as a whole, as step 4 is.

## 5. Performance

Sources: [Performance: does v6 need a plan before the build?](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/288), [Routing to more than one target: what it costs](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/268).

v6 needs **no performance pass before the build**. It uses the free routing option (one field pair, every target miner a seed), and the larger map doesn't raise the per-tick cost.

### Where v5 stands

On the M5 with the release build, v5 met all three targets: its worst real wave-20 tick was 12.1 ms against a 16.7 ms frame, leaving about 4.6 ms. Field settling is budgeted at `SETTLE_CELLS_PER_TICK` (900) cells a tick across both fields, about 1.5 ms.

### Where v6 adds cost

- **Several seeds** cost nothing per tick: it is still one field pair, and each enemy still samples one field. A seed change costs one full async rebuild of the pair on the worker threads (about 17 ms of worker time on v5's map, about 1.5× that on the v6 map), swapped in 12 ticks later.
- **The larger map** (136×96, about 1.5× v5's area) lengthens full builds and settling, but the per-tick repair is capped by the budget, so the tick cost doesn't grow with it.
- **Four spawn edges** spread the crowd more widely; the cost per enemy doesn't change.
- **Mining, threat and the alert** are a handful of numbers a tick.
- **Repairing soldiers** adds soldiers to each drone's pick, a few dozen candidates at most.
- **Uncapped waves:** a long run grows past 1,000, and that is allowed. Larger waves are measured for information only.

### What counts as done

v5's targets keep their thresholds. All on the M5 with the release build, with jumpers, brutes and perching in the bench's crowd and the army in the field:

| Case | Target |
|---|---|
| 1,000 dense (`tools/bench_frame.tscn`, `tools/bench_profile.tscn`) | frame p99 ≤ 16.7 ms, so **tick p99 ≤ about 12 ms** |
| 1,390 dense | frame **mean** ≤ 16.7 ms; the p99 may go over |
| A real wave of about 1,000 on the v6 map at High, with the base and two running rigs (four edges, three targets) | no tick over 16.7 ms |
| A larger real wave (about 1,500) | **reported for information only**, no pass or fail |

The decision named the real wave "about 1,000 at threat 18". By the starting numbers, threat 18 at High with two rigs is about 1,420 before the roll, so the bench sets the threat for the size instead: the real wave runs at High with two mining rigs and the roll off (`waves.size_roll` 0), at threat 16.4 for about 1,000 and 18.2 for about 1,500.

### Order

1. **The bench moves to the v6 map** in the threat ticket, which retires the wave number it jumped to: `bench_sim` and `BenchCrowd` run on `v6.json`, and `WAVE := 20` becomes a threat and a pace. **The rig ticket adds a rig as a second seed**, switched on and off every 600 ticks to exercise reseeding; `bench_sim` checks that the rebuild swapped in and that enemies reached the rig. The decision asked for one ticket; it takes two because the threat ticket can't keep a wave number the bench jumps to, and the rig doesn't exist until later. Each of those two PRs loses its relative comparison once, and says so.
2. **Per-ticket checks:** every ticket that touches enemies, overflow, piles, buildings, routing, waves or the tick driver pastes `bench_profile` dense-1000, with and without the army, in its PR's Performance section. The rig ticket also reports the rebuild's swap wait under switching.
3. **The v6 performance re-measure** comes after the rig, soldier repair, extraction and vein tickets and before QA. It measures the targets on the finished build, posts the tables, and files follow-up tickets only if a target is missed.

### Windows

There is no Windows work in v6. Nothing in v6 raises the per-tick worst case beyond the crowd itself, but three things matter on the PC, and the Windows wayfinding session after the prototype picks them up: full field builds take about 1.5× as long on the larger map, on fewer and slower cores; each rig change triggers one; and uncapped waves make "past 1,000" a normal part of a long run rather than a stress test. The `bench-pc` flow can bench v6 PRs on the PC as it did v5's, for information.

## 6. Map, deposits and routing

Sources: [The v6 map](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/283), [Deposits and outposts](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/272), [Performance: does v6 need a plan before the build?](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/288).

### The v6 map

- **`data/maps/v6.json`**, hand-made, about **136×96** cells. **All four edges spawn.** The base (4×4) sits in a central bowl ringed by rock. Each edge's route keeps v5's flare: a wide, ragged mouth narrowing to a chokepoint into the bowl, with small overflow pockets beside it. `:` marks the no-build margin, 6 cells deep along every spawn edge. The shape may be tuned during the build if it plays better.
- **Deposits:** three besides the base's, fixed in the map data, each a 3×3 area in a **basin**: a side space opening off one route's flare through a single mouth, apart from the overflow pockets.
  - **Near (W):** just outside the W route's chokepoint, 720 gold and 30 Aether, 1–2 pylons from the base.
  - **Far, gold-rich (E):** off the E route, 2,000 gold and 100 Aether, 4–5 pylons from the base, clear of the no-build margin.
  - **Far, Aether-rich (N):** off the N route, 1,440 gold and 360 Aether, 4–5 pylons from the base, clear of the no-build margin.
  - The S route has none. The base's own deposit holds 4,200 gold and 1,400 Aether.
- **Pylon chains follow the routes:** the rock between routes and basins is too thick for a chain to cross, so the only clear cells a chain can step through lie where waves walk. The exact cells are settled when the map is drawn, and a test pins the chain lengths (section 17).
- The sketch it was chosen from is on [The v6 map](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/283). `v1.json`, `v2-narrow.json` and `v3-flared.json` stay selectable from the debug panel.

### The map format

- **Version 2** adds `base_deposit` (`{"gold": 4200, "aether": 1400}`) and `deposits`, a list of `{"name", "origin", "gold", "aether"}`, each a 3×3 area from its origin. A deposit's cells must be clear, inside the map, outside the no-build margin and not overlap the base or another deposit; the loader rejects a map that breaks this, as it does a bad base.
- **Version 1** maps still load, with no rig deposits and the base's deposit at the v6 contents (constants in `MapData`), so the older maps play under v6's rules.
- `MapData.deposit_at(cell)` names the deposit a cell belongs to, if any.

### Structures

Unchanged from v5. Walls, buildings (rigs among them) and the base are structures under one rule: an enemy attacks one only when it is in the way (its route crosses it within 2 steps and it is pressed against it, or it is jammed against it), at 2 HP/s, or 10 HP/s for a brute. A wall has 15 HP and drops to a level-3 pile at 0; a destroyed building or rig leaves nothing; the base has 200 HP and the run is lost at 0. Perched enemies attack the building, rig or base they stand on. Soldiers are not structures.

### Routing

- **Two fields as in v5**, **sensible** and **direct**, with true shortest-path parents, octile steps with no corner cutting, finite costs for walls and buildings by their time to break, incremental updates under the per-tick settling budget that equal a full rebuild exactly once settled, route rolls (5% pile, 3% wall or building), wave-tail push-through (turn-back angle 90°), lifted enemies taking no part in routing, and jumpers and brutes locked to the direct route.
- **Seeds:** both fields are seeded from every **target miner**: the base's cells, always, and the cells of each rig that is **running** (switched on and powered), dry or not. An enemy follows its field to whichever target is cheapest by route cost, so walling a rig off pushes its attackers on to the next nearest, which may be the base. A rig's cells are seeded at `routing.base_bias` (0) rather than 0, so a positive bias makes the base preferred; at 0 the nearest wins outright.
- **Seed changes:** when `Miners.take_seed_change()` reports a change at step 1, `Routing.set_seeds` starts a **full async rebuild** of both fields on the worker threads, with the new seeds, swapped in 12 ticks later. Pending incremental repairs are folded into it. Only one rebuild is in flight: a seed change during one becomes a single follow-up rebuild started when it swaps in. A decay rebuild due at the same time is the same rebuild.
- **Changes during a rebuild:** cells changed while a rebuild is in flight (bodies landing, walls breaking, buildings built) are **kept** and fed to `Routing.update` as soon as it swaps in, then settled under the budget as usual. A rebuild so always swaps in after 12 ticks, even mid-wave with bodies landing every tick. v5's path, which restarted the builds on any change, could keep a rebuild from ever finishing in a busy wave, and decay being off is all that hid it.
- **Target of a cell:** `Miners.target_of(cell)` walks the sensible parents from a cell to a seed cell and returns the miner it belongs to. It is called a few times per field swap (once per edge of the coming wave), never per enemy.

## 7. Mining and the pace

Sources: [How the player controls the mining pace](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/267), [The mining economy](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/270), [Starting numbers for mining, threat and the quota](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/287).

- **Gold is the only currency** and comes only from mining. Kills pay nothing; there is no bounty and no combo. Starting gold (300) and every v5 cost stay.
- **Minerals:** gold and Aether. Gold is spent; Aether can't be, and counts towards the quota (section 10).
- **The pace:** one setting on the base, `OFF`, `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, set by `SetPace` at any moment, mid-wave included. It starts at `OFF`: the player chooses when the first wave is drawn. The pace sets every miner; a rig has only its switch.
- **A miner is mining** when it is not dry, the pace is above Off, the siege hasn't started and, for a rig, it is running. Damage never slows it, and repair doesn't stop it.
- **The rate:** each mining miner mines `mining.base_rate × income(pace)` minerals a second (3.2 at High, ×0.3 / ×0.5 / ×1 by pace), a rig at `rigs.rate` (×0.5) of that, in equal shares each tick.
- **The mix:** a deposit's starting contents are its mix. Each tick's minerals split in proportion to them (the base 75% gold and 25% Aether), so every mineral in a deposit runs out on the same tick. A share is capped at what is left; once nothing is left the miner is **dry** and stays dry.
- **Paid in small, frequent amounts:** mined gold adds to a fractional total, and `RunState.gold` gains each whole gold as the total passes it, so the gold readout ticks up steadily. Aether and the per-kind totals are kept fractional and shown rounded down.
- **Threat counts every mineral** (gold, Aether and vein gold) from every miner (section 8).
- **A dry base:** once the base is dry and no rig is both running and not dry, `Miners.all_dry()` is true: the pace is locked (`SetPace` is rejected and the switch greys out), and the alert fills and waves are sized by the dry rule (section 8), whatever the pace. While a rig still has something to mine and is running, the pace still sets it and the alert follows the pace.
- **The readout:** `Miners.gold_rate()` is the gold a second at the current pace across every mining miner, for the HUD's Mining row.

### Rich veins

Sources: [Starting numbers for mining, threat and the quota](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/287).

- Each tick, a mining miner rolls for a **rich vein**: the base at High with `veins.chance` (8%) a minute, at Medium a quarter of that (`veins.medium`), at Low never; a rig at `veins.rig` (×0.1) of the base's chance at the same pace. The per-tick chance is the minute's divided by 3,600. A dry miner strikes none.
- A vein pays `veins.gold` (400) gold at once, extra to the deposit: what is left in it doesn't change. It holds no Aether. It counts towards threat like any mineral (400 ÷ 360, about +1.1).
- Rolls come from `Miners`' own seeded RNG, so a run replays exactly. `vein_struck(miner_id, gold)` drives the toast.

## 8. Threat, the alert and the waves

Sources: [How enemies arrive](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/269), [Rules that hung on the build phase](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/275), [Prior art: income that draws enemies](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/266), [Starting numbers for mining, threat and the quota](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/287).

### Threat

- A run starts at threat `threat.start` (1). Each tick adds `1 ÷ (threat.seconds_per_point × 60)` (1 point per 1,000 s) and every mineral mined adds `1 ÷ threat.minerals_per_point` (1 per 360). It never falls. Kills play no part.
- At High the base alone gains about 1 point every 100 s, reaching 18 in about 29 minutes; mining is about 90% of it. Low takes about 2.7× as long; at Off a point takes about 17 minutes.
- **Every v5 rule that read the wave number reads threat:** wave *n* means threat *n*. Continuous rules (size, spawn rate, burst size, scatter share) read the threat as it stands when the wave starts; stepped rules (spawn edges, when special enemies join and their steps, the wave tail) read its whole points (`Threat.whole()`).

### The alert

- The alert fills from 0 to 1 while no wave is open (the **quiet**), at `1 ÷ (alert.fill_seconds × 60)` a tick (60 s at High) times the pace's fill: ×0.6 at Low, ×0.8 at Medium, ×1 at High. At Off it doesn't fill, and holds where it is. However many rigs are mining, its speed is the same.
- **Dry:** while `Miners.all_dry()`, it fills at `alert.dry_fill` (×0.8) whatever the pace, so the late game never goes quiet.
- **At 75%** (`alert.warn_at`), the next wave's spawn edges are picked and `wave_coming(edges)` fires, for the highlight and the toast. Once picked they stay picked for that wave, even if the pace drops to Off.
- **Full:** a wave starts, and the alert empties. It stays empty until that wave's last enemy dies, so **waves never overlap** and every wave is followed by a quiet. The siege is the one exception (section 10).

### A wave

- **Size**, fixed when it starts: `round(wave_1_size × growth^(threat − 1) × (pace factor + rigs.size_bonus × mining rigs) × roll)`, at least 1. The pace factor is the pace's size (×0.7 at Low, ×1 at Medium, ×1.4 at High) as the wave starts, or `waves.dry_size` (×1) while dry; each rig mining as it starts adds 0.1, so two rigs at High make ×1.6. The **roll** is uniform within ±`size_roll` (15%), fixed per run and wave (seeded by `hash([seed, "wave_roll", index])`, where the index counts waves started this run). **No cap.**
- **Enemy HP** is each kind's HP × `hp_growth^(threat − 1)`, and `hp_growth` is 1, so HP is flat: 10, a bloater 30, a brute 40.
- **Spawn edges:** 1, then 2 from threat 6, 3 from 10 and 4 from 15 (whole points, read when they are picked), capped at the map's spawn edges, drawn at random without repeats from the waves RNG. A wave that starts without them picked (a `SendWave` before 75%) picks them as it starts.
- **Special enemies**, from whole threat as v5 read the wave: bloaters from threat 5, a base of `1 + (threat − 5) ÷ 6`, rolled for one fewer (25%) or one more (10%); jumpers from threat 8, `round(size × 5%)`, capped at `jumpers.cap` when above 0; brutes from threat 12, a base of `1 + (threat − 12) ÷ 6` with their own roll. Rolls are seeded by the wave's index, not its threat (`hash([seed, "bloaters", index])`, `"brutes"`), so two waves at the same threat roll apart. Placement is v5's: random slots of the plan, drawn without replacement, bloaters, then jumpers, then brutes, in place of normal enemies.
- **The spawn plan** is v5's, reading the threat at the wave's start: `S = round(N × share)` scattered slots and the rest in bursts of `round(16 × 1.05^(threat − 1))`, the share running in a straight line from 90% at threat 1 to 10% at threat 12 and holding; spawning at `10 × 1.1^(threat − 1)` enemies a second; each burst on a stretch of its edge (edges in turn), `6 × sqrt(b ÷ 16)` cells wide; scattered enemies alone on a random spawn cell of a random one of the wave's edges, with ±0.25 cells of jitter.
- **Enemies left** is the open waves' enemies not yet killed, unspawned ones included. It shows only during a wave.
- **The wave tail:** once a wave's enemies left falls to `10 + 2 × whole threat` (the threat when it started), its enemies may push through to the direct route as in v5.
- **A wave ends** when all its enemies have spawned and none is alive, perched ones included. Trickle enemies and debug spawns never count. Soldiers don't count.
- **The pace** can change mid-wave and takes effect on the mining and the alert at once; it never changes a wave already spawning.

### The trickle

- A steady stream of scattered **normal** enemies, from the start of the run, at every pace, Off included, quiet and wave alike: one every `trickle.interval` (2.5 s) at first, rate × `trickle.growth^(threat − 1)` (×1.05 a point of threat). Each tick adds the rate to its own budget, and each whole enemy spawns alone on a random spawn cell of a random spawn edge of the map, with the same jitter.
- Trickle enemies belong to no wave (`wave` = `-1`): they don't count towards Enemies left or any wave's end, and they always follow the wave-tail rule. There are no special enemies in it.

### The quiet

- The time from a wave's end to the next wave, while the alert refills and the trickle keeps coming. At Off it lasts until the pace is raised. It replaces the build phase: there is no Next wave, no instant training and no recovery at a wave's end.
- **A wave's end** still triggers pile decay (still off). During the siege, where waves overlap, it fires only once no wave enemies are left.

## 9. Rigs

Sources: [Deposits and outposts](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/272), [Starting numbers for mining, threat and the quota](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/287), [HUD: pace, alert and the end of a run](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/286).

- **A rig** is a building that mines the deposit it is built on: 3×3, 250 gold, 150 HP, power area 10 (the base's), perch room 2 per cell (18), hotkey **M**. It follows every building rule: it is built on the power grid, blocks routes, is attacked, perched on, blasted and repaired like any building, and leaves nothing when destroyed.
- **Placement:** a rig's footprint must cover a deposit exactly, one rig to a deposit. While placing a rig, the cursor snaps to the deposit under it. Other buildings can't cover a deposit's cells, so a deposit stays free for its rig. Rejections add "not on a deposit" (a rig off one) and "reserved for a rig" (another building on one); the rest are v5's ("off the power grid", "occupied", "not enough gold", ...).
- **The switch:** on when built. `SetRigSwitch` turns it off or on at any time, mid-wave included, with no cooldown. A rig is **running** while it is switched on and powered (on the grid; repair doesn't matter). A running rig is a **target**, dry or not, so a dry rig left running is a **decoy**. Switched off or unpowered, it mines nothing and enemies don't head for it, so a cut pylon chain takes it out of their sights.
- **Mining:** while running and not dry, at the pace, at half the base's rate (1.6 minerals a second at High), in its deposit's mix.
- **The cost in enemies:** each rig mining as a wave starts adds `rigs.size_bonus` (+0.1) to that wave's size factor, added, not compounded; its minerals raise threat like the base's, so threat rises about 45% faster with one rig at High. A rig doesn't speed up the alert.
- **Losing one** costs only itself: its mined gold stays banked, the deposit keeps what is left, and a new rig can be built on it at full price.
- **Wave targets:** while a wave is coming (edges picked) or open, `wave_targets` names the miner each of its edges leads to, from `Miners.target_of` at the middle spawn cell of each edge. It is recomputed when the edges are picked and whenever the fields swap in. The view marks each with the pulsing ring.

## 10. Ending a run

Sources: [How a run ends](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/271), [Starting numbers for mining, threat and the quota](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/287).

- **Two outcomes:** **Base destroyed** when the base's HP reaches 0, and **Extracted** when the siege's countdown runs out with the base standing. No endless mode and no automatic win. If both happen on one tick, the base's loss wins.
- **The quota:** once the Aether mined (from every miner) reaches `extraction.quota` (1,100), `Extraction` turns `READY`, `quota_met` fires and Extract lights up. At High the base alone meets it around threat 14.75, after about 23 minutes, at 79% of its Aether.
- **Extract** commits the player: there is no cancel. On the step it is drained:
  - every miner stops (rigs included), so no rig adds to the siege's waves, and the pace locks;
  - a countdown of `extraction.countdown` (75 s, 4,500 ticks) starts;
  - a **final wave** starts at once at High's size factor (×1.4) and the current threat, with no rig bonus, its own roll and freshly picked edges;
  - the alert empties and refills at `extraction.alert_fill` (×1.5) High's speed (40 s), **whether or not a wave is open**; each fill starts another wave at High's size, so waves may overlap, and each picks its edges at 75%;
  - the trickle runs `extraction.trickle` (×3) as fast.
- Killing faster doesn't shorten the siege. Threat still rises with time. Running rigs stay targets, since they are still switched on and powered.
- **Staying on:** past the quota the player can keep mining. A dry base keeps drawing waves by the dry rule and never forces the player out.
- **Yield:** on Extracted, every mineral mined this run, of every kind (gold including veins, and Aether). On Base destroyed, 0, with what was mined struck through.
- **After the end:** `run_ended(outcome)` fires once, the sim keeps ticking for the view's sake as v5 did after a game over, and only a restart is accepted.

## 11. Enemies, piles, overflow and perching

Unchanged from v5 except that no enemy pays bounty, HP is flat, and where a route ends at a rig rather than the base. Sources: `docs/specs/v5.md` sections 7, 9–12.

- **Kinds and stats:** normal (10 HP, 2 cells/s), bloater (30 HP, 1.6 cells/s, ×1.5 size, 1 s fuse, 2-cell blast of 30), jumper (10 HP, 2.4 cells/s, purple), brute (40 HP, 1.4 cells/s, mass 3, ×5 structure damage, ×1.8 size, 2-level body). HP doesn't grow with threat (section 8).
- **Storage and movement:** packed arrays and a spatial hash (ADR 0001), a per-enemy state (`WALKING`, `TUMBLING`, `CROUCHING`, `JUMPING`, `PERCHED`) and, new in v6, a per-enemy wave index. Separation weighted by mass, personal space, collision, jam detection, pace (±3%), wander and heading offset, one neighbour scan over the 3×3 buckets in `NeighbourScan`. Piles slow every kind 15/30/45/60%.
- **Kill credit:** `Enemies.damage(id, amount, by)` still records the last hitter, and the death list still carries it, though nothing is paid on it. **Kills** counts every enemy death in the run.
- **Targeting** by towers and soldiers is unchanged: `bloaters_first` (the default) or `closest_to_base`, which ranks by the sensible field's value at the enemy's position. With several seeds that value is the route cost to the nearest target miner, so it reads "closest to its target"; the toggle's label becomes **Closest to target** and its key stays.
- **Piles and walls:** five levels, then a 15 HP wall; bodies land where their enemy died or the nearest valid cell; a broken wall drops to level 3; a brute's body adds 2 levels; decay off; only a blast erases.
- **Overflow:** barriers, the 3-cell band, the gauge (fill 1.5 per stalled enemy a second, leak 40, clamp 0–120, active above 10 until below 4), tiers (trickle 20, stream 50, surge 90) launching tumbles from the front rank at 180% speed. Brutes, crouching or jumping jumpers and lifted enemies are never picked.
- **Tumble planning** walks v5's straight plan, then the direct field's parent chain, now to whichever target miner it leads to: a building (rigs included) with room ends it perched there; a wall or full building is crossed; a target miner's cells end it as the base's did, perched on it if it has room, else on the free cell beside it nearest where it reached it; else the first clear or pile cell with fewer than 6 enemies ends it on the ground.
- **Jumps** are v5's: a ready jumper that would start attacking a structure crouches 0.25 s, flies 0.8 s over at most 3 wall and building cells, and lands perched on a building with room or on the ground beyond, with a 4 s cooldown. It ends on a target miner as it did on the base, perched if there is room, and never jumps over one.
- **Perched enemies:** 2 per footprint cell on a building or rig, 2 per cell on the base, room claimed at planning; each attacks what it stands on at 2 HP/s; grinders hurt their own; released walking when their building or rig is destroyed; bodies drop to their drop-off cell. A rig's perched enemies drop off when it falls, like any building's.
- **Blasts:** every living enemy within 2 cells dies with no body; every building, rig and the base within 2 cells takes 30 once per blast; piles and walls within 2 cells are erased; soldiers within 2 cells take 30.
- **Route lock:** a jumper or brute stays on the direct route, landing back on it; every other enemy goes back to the sensible route after a tumble or a drop.

## 12. Buildings, power, repair, towers and grinders

Unchanged from v5, with the rig and with soldiers under repair.

| Kind | Footprint | Cost | HP | Power area | Perch room | Hotkey |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tower | 2×2 | 120 | 60 | 4 | 8 | T |
| Grinder | 2×2 | 90 | 100 | 2 | 8 | G |
| Pylon | 1×1 | 30 | 20 | 10 | 2 | P |
| Repair yard | 2×2 | 120 | 60 | 4 | 8 | R |
| Barracks | 2×2 | 200 | 100 | 3 | 8 | B |
| **Rig** | **3×3** | **250** | **150** | **10** | **18** | **M** |
| Base (not a building) | 4×4 | – | 200 | 10 | 32 | – |

- **Power:** square power areas from the footprint's edge (Chebyshev), powered through overlaps outwards from the always-powered base, recomputed whenever the set of buildings changes. A powered rig powers its area like any building. An unpowered building stops working but stays a structure.
- **Working:** powered, and not under repair unless it is a pylon. A rig's mining reads **running** instead (section 9).
- **Placement (step 0):** every footprint cell in bounds, not no-build, on the power grid and clear (no rock, base, building or pile), not on a deposit unless it is a rig placed exactly on one, then the barracks limit, then gold. Building is allowed at any time, paused included. No selling or demolishing.
- **Towers:** 5 damage, 3.5 shots/s, range 8, firing in build order, targeting per tower. **Grinders:** 3 HP/s to every walking enemy touching them and every enemy perched on them, 3 at once per grinder cell a tumble or jump enters.

### Repair yards

Sources: [Rules that hung on the build phase](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/275).

- One drone each, straight flight at 8 cells/s, 5 HP/s until full, repair area 8 (Chebyshev from the yard's footprint), a threshold of 25/50/75/100% (50% when built), one assignment.
- **Whenever powered:** drones work at any time their yard is powered, waves or not; the fly-home at a wave's end goes. A drone whose yard loses power flies home and stops, as in v5.
- **Soldiers:** a soldier inside the repair area whose HP is below the threshold is a target like a building. Auto picks from one pool of buildings and soldiers: nearest the yard first, then lowest HP, then lowest id, preferring one no other drone targets. A soldier can be the yard's assignment (`Assign` with its id).
- **Following:** a drone repairing a soldier flies to the soldier's current position each tick and repairs while within 0.5 cells of it. If the soldier leaves the repair area or dies, the drone picks again.
- **Under repair:** a soldier being repaired can move and take orders, but holds fire and keeps its cooldown, as a tower does. Drones never repair walls, the base or themselves.

## 13. Soldiers

Unchanged from v5 except that the build phase and recovery are gone. `docs/specs/v4.md` sections 9–14 hold the full record of how v4 built them.

- **Barracks and training:** a 2×2 building (200 gold, 100 HP, power area 3), at most 5. Capacity 10 per barracks standing. `Train(barracks_id, count)` queues 1 or 5 at 30 gold each; `CancelTraining` refunds. **Training always takes 5 s a soldier** while the barracks is working, at any time; instant training and training at a wave's end go. A destroyed barracks refunds its queue. A rally point gives each new soldier a Move to it.
- **Soldiers, orders and fighting:** as in v5. 45 HP, 2 damage at 2 shots/s, range 4 plus 0.5 a level of elevation, 2.5 cells/s, mass 5; `Move` in Cluster or Line, `Stop`; contact by mass; knock-off by tumbles and jumps. Kills pay nothing.
- **No recovery:** a soldier keeps its damage from wave to wave and is healed only by a repair yard's drone (section 12), so at Off a defence without a yard slowly wears down. A newly trained soldier starts at full HP.
- **Death:** a soldier at 0 HP is removed at step 9, freeing capacity, and leaves one body unless a blast killed it.

## 14. Run state and stats

- **Run state:** base HP 200 and gold 300 at the start. Gold comes from mining and veins and is spent on buildings and soldiers. Kills counts every enemy death.
- **Minerals mined:** per kind (gold, Aether), from every miner, veins included, kept fractional.
- **Stats for the Debrief:** the threat reached, the run's time in ticks, the kills, the biggest wave (its size), the ticks spent at each pace before Extract, and the siege's ticks. The outcome and the Yield (section 10).

Restart is its one button.

## 17. Testing, CI and performance

Sources: [Testing, CI and performance checks](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/10), [Performance: does v6 need a plan before the build?](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/288).

### The test seam

- **The Simulation is the seam**, as in v5: build a `Simulation` on a tiny inline map (`TestMaps.from_rows`, which gains deposits) with fixed settings and seed, queue commands, run ticks, and assert on public state and end-of-tick signals.
- New public state to assert on: the pace and whether it is locked; each miner's remaining contents, mining, running, target and dry state; gold and minerals credited per tick; threat by time and minerals; the alert's fill by pace, dry and siege; when edges are picked and how many; each wave's size from threat, pace, rigs and roll; special counts by threat and wave index; each enemy's wave index; Enemies left per wave; wave end with the trickle excluded; the trickle's rate; the field seeds and which miner a cell's route ends at; rebuilds started by seed changes, swapped in after 12 ticks despite changes landing during them, and merged when they overlap; veins struck by chance and paid; the quota, Extract's rejections, the siege's waves overlapping, the countdown and the outcome; the Yield; the run stats; drones repairing soldiers, following them and soldiers holding fire under repair; training always 5 s; no recovery.
- **Kept seams:** the routing exactness test stays at `Routing`, now with several seeds; the scan comparison test as in v5; `bench_sim` and the local benchmarks stay the performance seam.
- **The map test:** `v6.json` loads; every spawn edge reaches the base; each deposit is in a basin with one mouth (its region off the route is enclosed but for one opening); and the fewest pylons that put each deposit on the grid, found by a breadth-first search over clear, buildable cells by the power-area rule, is 1–2 for the near deposit and 4–5 for each far one.
- Each build ticket's acceptance criteria name the tests it adds, written test-first. Rendering, input and UI (deposits, the rig, the alert strip, the pace switch, toasts, the target ring, the green cross, the Debrief) have no automated tests; the human checks them in playtests.

### The bench

- **The threat ticket** moves `bench_sim` and `BenchCrowd` to `v6.json` and replaces `WAVE := 20` with `JumpToThreat` and pace High; `BenchCrowd` keeps topping up its crowd with jumpers, brutes and perching as in v5, from all four edges.
- **The rig ticket** builds two rigs (near and one far) for the real-wave case and switches one on and off every 600 ticks; `bench_sim` checks that the rebuild swapped in and that enemies reached a rig.
- The PRs that change the bench lose their relative comparison that one time, and say so.

### CI

CI keeps `lint`, `test`, `sim-budget` and `export`, all required on every PR. `sim-budget` stays at 1.2× the base's best tick mean on the same runner. Its 80 ms backstop is rechecked on the v6 map in the threat ticket and changed only with a reason. The native library is rebuilt by the existing workflow when `native/` changes.

## 19. Build tickets

Each ticket is test-first and ends in something the human can run and check. Tickets are built on the `v6-build` integration branch, cut from `main` once this spec merges: each on its own `feat/*` branch, playtested by the human, then merged by PR into `v6-build`. One umbrella PR takes `v6-build` into `main` at the end. They are built in this order, each blocked by the tickets noted, and tracked as sub-issues of [Prototype v6 build](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/292) with native blocking edges.
