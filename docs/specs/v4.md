# Prototype v4 spec: units

This spec gathers every technical decision for prototype v4 in one place and ends in the ordered list of build tickets. v4 asks one question: **do units the player directs give a satisfying answer to leaks and floods, without turning pile-up into an RTS?** In a v3 playtest a few enemies got behind the base, and the only answer was a turret the player didn't want there. v4 adds the **soldier**, trained in the **barracks** and directed StarCraft-style, with **elevation** tying soldiers to piles. It opens with a **performance pass** towards the 1,000-enemy target, which v3 missed, and keeps about 2 ms of the tick for units.

It is self-contained: where v4 keeps a v3 rule, the rule is restated here, and `docs/specs/v3.md` stays frozen as history. Game rules and their starting numbers come from `docs/design.md`; vocabulary from `GLOSSARY.md`. If this spec and a closed ticket disagree, this spec is current. Planned in [Map: prototype v4 spec](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/156).

## 1. Platform and engine

Unchanged from v3.

- Godot 4.7.2, standard build, typed GDScript, pinned in one CI workflow variable.
- Platforms: macOS and Windows. Baseline machine: Apple M5 MacBook, 16 GB RAM.
- Performance target: about 1,000 enemies on screen at 60 fps on the baseline machine, measured on the **release** build (the official export template), since the debug build adds about 40% to the tick. Section 5 sets what counts as done.
- No C#. A hot loop that has to leave GDScript moves to a C++ GDExtension behind the same module interface (ADR 0001), and only if section 5's re-measure says so.

## 2. Architecture

Sources: ADR 0001, ADR 0002, [Code organisation and system boundaries](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/11).

The v3 architecture stands: a node-free `Simulation` owns every system, their seeded RNGs and the tick driver; the view draws it and turns input into commands; nothing under `src/sim/` extends `Node` or `Resource` or references `src/view/`, and the `lint` job enforces it. Commands drain at step 0, so a seed plus a list of (tick, command) pairs replays a run exactly. Systems never signal each other; the tick driver passes data between them. A restart builds a fresh `Simulation`. Every building is in one `Buildings` registry, and enemies attack walls, buildings and the base through the `Structures` facade. Kill credit is the enemy's last hitter, carried on the death list.

### What changes in v4

- **Soldiers are stored apart from enemies.** A new `Soldiers` system keeps them in packed arrays, as `Enemies` does (ADR 0001), but in its own system: soldiers aren't enemies, never count towards a wave, Enemies left or Kills, and an army of 50 doesn't need the enemies' hot loop to branch on a kind. `Soldiers` keeps no spatial hash of its own: at 50 soldiers, soldier-to-soldier checks are a plain pairwise pass (about 1,200 pairs), and soldier-to-enemy contact reads the enemies' hash from the soldier side (section 10), so the enemy loop gains no work per enemy.
- **One id space for killers.** Buildings and soldiers draw their ids from one counter, `Ids`, owned by `Simulation` and handed to both. `Enemies.last_hitters`, the death list's killers, `Combos` and `RunState.pay` then work for soldiers unchanged: a soldier's id is a killer like a tower's.
- **Barracks** are a fifth building kind. Training queues, capacity and the barracks limit live in a new `Barracks` system beside `RepairYards`.
- **Unit pathing** is a new `UnitPaths` system: one field per Move order and per rally point, settled under a per-tick cell budget, never the enemies' flow fields (section 11).
- **Enemies** gain a soldier target per enemy (the soldier it is attacking, or `NONE`), held like a structure target. A tumble that crosses a wall cell reports it, so `Soldiers` can knock off whoever stands there.
- **Routing** settles its fields under a per-tick budget (section 5).
- **The paused drain.** While paused, every queued command drains at once (not only time controls), as step 0 of the next tick run early: builds appear, gold changes and orders are taken while the clock is stopped, and they play out on resume. Replays are unchanged, since nothing else runs between that drain and the tick's own step 0.

### Systems

New and changed systems; the rest are as in v3.

| System | Owns | Interface (main parts) |
|---|---|---|
| `Ids` | The next killer id | `next() -> int`, used by `Buildings` and `Soldiers` |
| `Enemies` | As in v3, plus a soldier target per enemy | as in v3; `living_within`, `touching` and the hash read by `Soldiers` |
| `Soldiers` | Packed soldier arrays: id, position, HP, spot, order, path cursor, targeting mode, cooldown, current target, kills, blast-killed flag | `add(cell) -> id`, `move(ids, order)`, `stop(ids)`, `set_targeting(ids, mode)`, `step(enemies, knock_offs)` (step 5), `fire(enemies)` (step 7), `damage(id, amount, by_blast)`, `living_within(pos, radius)`, `push_out_of(footprint)`, `recover_all()`, `remove_dead() -> SoldierDeaths`, `elevation(index)` |
| `Barracks` | Per barracks: queue (up to 5), training timer, rally point; capacity and the count of barracks | `train(barracks_id, count)`, `cancel(barracks_id, slot)`, `set_rally(barracks_id, cell)`, `clear_rally(barracks_id)`, `capacity()`, `used()`, `step(phase)` (step 2), `finish_queues()` (at wave end), `remove(barracks_id)` (refunds) |
| `UnitPaths` | Live fields (one per Move order and per rally point), walkable regions, the settle queue | `plan(anchor, soldier_cells, slots) -> field_id`, `step()` (settles up to the budget), `path(field_id, from_cell, slot_cell)`, `update(changed_cells)`, `drop(field_id)` |
| `Routing` | Both flow fields, now with pending copies | as in v3; `update(cells)` feeds the pending fields, `step()` settles them under the budget and swaps them in when done |
| `Overflow` | As in v3 | as in v3, plus `take_knock_offs()`: each wall cell a tumble crossed this tick and that tumble's landing cell |
| `Blasts` | Nothing between ticks | `resolve(deaths, soldiers) -> deaths` also damages soldiers in each blast |
| `Buildings` | Every building in build order | as in v3, with `barracks`; `build_error` adds the barracks limit |

### Commands (new and changed)

- `Build(kind, origin)` accepts `barracks`.
- `Train(barracks_id, count)`, with `count` 1 or 5. `CancelTraining(barracks_id, slot)`.
- `SetRallyPoint(barracks_id, cell)`, `ClearRallyPoint(barracks_id)`.
- `Move(soldier_ids, target, formation)`, with `target` a map position and `formation` `cluster` or `line`. The formation rides in the command, read from the command card when the order is given, so replays are exact.
- `Stop(soldier_ids)`.
- `SetSoldierTargeting(soldier_ids, mode)`, with `mode` `bloaters_first` or `closest_to_base`.
- Debug: `AddSoldiers(count)` puts soldiers beside the base for free, ignoring capacity, so QA and the benches can field an army without barracks.

Selection, control groups and the Formation setting are the view's; only orders reach the simulation.

### Signals out to the view (end of tick)

v3's signals stand, and v4 adds:

- `soldiers_added(ids)` for soldiers trained or added this tick.
- `soldiers_died(deaths)` with each dead soldier's id and position, for the death effect and for the view to drop it from the selection and every control group.
- `shots_fired(shots)` carries soldiers' shots too, each with its origin position, for tracers.
- `training_changed(barracks_id)` when a queue, timer start or rally point changes.

Soldier positions, HP, elevation, combos, orders and paths change every tick, so the view reads them read-only each frame, as the enemy renderer reads the enemy arrays.

## 3. Tick order

The v3 order with the routing budget at step 1, training at step 2, and two new unit steps.

0. Drain commands (builds, trains, rally points, orders, targeting, assignments, thresholds, next wave, restart, settings, debug actions). A successful build recomputes power at once and pushes any soldiers off its footprint. A Train in the build phase trains at once.
1. `Piles` lands bodies queued last tick. On the tick after a wave end, `Piles.decay()` and `Routing.start_rebuild()` run next. The step-1 change list (pile level changes, walls broken or erased, buildings built or destroyed, HP bucket changes) goes to `Routing.update` (into its pending fields), `Overflow.update` and `UnitPaths.update`. Then `Routing.step()` settles the pending fields under its budget and swaps them in once they are settled, and the decay rebuild swaps in once it is due.
2. `Waves` spawns, in clump bursts, bloaters among them. `Barracks.step` runs each working barracks' training timer during a wave; a soldier that finishes appears beside its barracks.
3. Rebuild the spatial hash.
4. Move enemies, as in v3, with an enemy that has a soldier target holding its ground like an attacker. Then `Overflow.step()`, which also records the wall cells its tumbles cross.
5. **Move units** (`UnitPaths.step()`, then `Soldiers.step`): settle path fields under their budget; knock off soldiers on crossed wall cells; walk soldiers along their paths or back to their spots; push soldiers and enemies apart by mass; space soldiers out; then find enemies jammed against soldiers, which start attacking them (or light their fuse, for a bloater), and apply this tick's attacks.
6. Buildings act, in build order: working towers fire, working grinders hurt every enemy touching them.
7. **Units fire** (`Soldiers.fire`), in soldier order.
8. Drones fly and repair (`RepairYards.step()`).
9. Remove dead enemies, then `Blasts.resolve` the deaths (enemies, buildings, the base, piles and soldiers), then remove dead soldiers (bodies, capacity, combos), then `Combos.expire(tick)` and `RunState.pay` each death in order. Bodies of deaths that leave one go to `Piles.queue_bodies`. Buildings destroyed by a blast leave at once and lose their combo; a destroyed barracks refunds its queue.
10. `Waves` checks for wave end; on wave end, decay becomes due at step 1 of the next tick, drones fly home, every living soldier recovers to full HP, every barracks' queue trains at once, and the phase becomes build phase.

Then the end-of-tick signals are emitted. Removal waits until step 9, so enemy and soldier ids and indices are stable for the whole tick. Soldiers move at step 5 and fire at step 7 as steps of their own, so the step profiler (section 18) reads the units' cost directly.

## 5. Performance pass

Sources: [v4: performance pass toward 1,000 enemies at 60 fps](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/150), [Profile a 1,000-enemy tick in the release build](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/157), [v4 performance plan](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/158). The pass is folded into v4: its tickets open the build.

### Where v3 stands

`tools/bench_frame.tscn` on the M5, 1,000 dense enemies, release build: tick 13.8 ms mean, 20–21 ms p99; frame CPU 16.0 ms mean, 22–24 ms p99, against a 16.7 ms budget. The engine's own work is nearly free (drawing about 0.2–0.3 ms, signals under 0.05 ms); the cost is GDScript running per-enemy loops.

- **The mean** goes to enemy movement. The steer loop is about 70% of the tick (9.3–9.7 ms), and about 8.5 ms of it is two neighbour scans over the same 3×3 buckets: separation (4.3–4.5 ms) and personal space (3.9–4.1 ms), since a personal space of 0.7 cells rounds up to the same scan. The collide, route and progress loop takes 2.1–2.4 ms.
- **The p99 spikes** come from re-settling the flow fields: nothing on most ticks, 5–21 ms on the 8–12% of ticks where a cell changes, because each change re-settles about 900–1,300 field cells across both routes. A worst tick is about 13 ms of movement plus 8–10 ms of settling. Overflow relabelling adds about 1.3 ms on those ticks.
- **Wave 20:** a dense 1,390 costs 20.2–20.7 ms mean, 22.4–27.2 ms p99; a real wave 20 against the bench's eight towers peaked at 930–994 alive, 4.6 ms mean, with field settling making its 21–22 ms worst ticks.

### What counts as done

All measured on the M5 with the release build:

| Case | Target |
|---|---|
| 1,000 dense (`tools/bench_frame.tscn`) | frame p99 ≤ 16.7 ms, so **tick p99 ≤ about 12 ms** |
| 1,390 dense (the wave-20 crowd) | frame **mean** ≤ 16.7 ms; the p99 may go over |
| A real wave 20 (jump to wave 20 with the bench's eight towers and an endless base, 7,200 ticks) | no tick over 16.7 ms |

**Headroom for units** is a time budget, not a unit count: about **2 ms of tick** at 1,000 dense, so units must fit under a tick p99 of about 14 ms with drawing (about 2 ms) taking the rest of the frame. Section 18 says how it is checked at a full army of 50.

### Order

1. **Merge separation and personal space** into one pass over the 3×3 buckets in `Enemies.move`, with no change in behaviour: the same pushes up to float rounding, and the current tests pass unchanged. About 4 ms of mean.
2. **Budgeted field settling.** `Routing.update(cells)` no longer settles in place: the change goes into a pending copy of both fields (invalidate and reseed as in v3), and `Routing.step()` settles at most `SETTLE_CELLS_PER_TICK` cells a tick across both fields, chosen so a tick's share costs about 1.5 ms on the M5. Once the pending heap is empty, both fields swap in together and `fields_changed` fires. Meanwhile enemies, overflow's tumble planning and the debug overlays read the old fields, so the crowd follows them for a few ticks. A change that arrives while fields are pending joins them rather than restarting. The budget is a cell count, not a time, so a run stays deterministic and replays exactly. Once settled, a field still equals a full rebuild exactly; the routing exactness test checks it after the swap. The decay rebuild on the worker thread is unchanged. Async settling on a worker and capping the ripple were weighed and passed over.
3. **Re-measure** against the targets above with the step profiler (section 18), and post the tables on the ticket.
4. **Only if a target is missed:** move `Enemies.move` (and the spatial hash, if it helps) to a C++ GDExtension behind the same module, as ADR 0001 plans, starting with a cheap probe that ports only the separation scan and benchmarks it. The re-measure ticket files that work if it's needed; it isn't filed now. A C# port is out: ADR 0001 rejected it for GC pauses, and the targets are desktop only.

### Behaviour changes are welcome

The pass may change how the crowd behaves if it **looks the same in play**, and such changes are encouraged once the exact optimisations are in: running personal space every other tick, lowering `neighbour_cap`, or the field lag from step 2. Each look-alike change is checked by eye in a run and noted in its ticket, and any test it changes is changed in that ticket.

## 6. Map, structures and routing

Unchanged from v3, except for the budgeted settling (section 5) and what soldiers add.

- **The map** is `data/maps/v3-flared.json` (112×76 cells, about 3,400 clear, spawn edges N, E and W with ragged flared mouths, a 4×4 base at (53, 54), four small pockets beside the chokepoints). `:` marks the no-build margin, 6 cells deep along every spawn edge. `v1.json` and `v2-narrow.json` stay selectable from the debug panel. The map format keeps version 1.
- **Structures:** walls, buildings and the base, under one rule. An enemy attacks one only when it is in the way: its route crosses it within 2 steps and it is pressed against it (within `separation_radius + wall_reach` of a structure cell's square), or it is jammed against it (no progress for `jam_seconds` while pressed). Every attacker deals 2 HP/s and holds its ground. A wall has 15 HP and drops to a level-3 pile at 0; a destroyed building leaves nothing; the base has 200 HP and the run ends at 0. A bloater lights its fuse where a normal enemy would attack. An enemy killed at a structure dies in front of it.
- **Soldiers are not structures.** They are never in `Occupancy`, never part of a barrier and never in the flow fields, so a soldier never makes the fields re-settle. Enemies attack a soldier by its own rule (section 12).
- **Routing:** two integration fields (**sensible** and **direct**) with true shortest-path parents, octile steps with no corner cutting, finite costs for walls and buildings by their time to break, no blocking check, incremental updates that equal a full rebuild exactly once settled, a worker rebuild after decay, route rolls (5% pile, 3% wall or building), wave-tail push-through (turn-back angle 90°), and tumbling enemies taking no part in routing. Barracks count as buildings.

## 7. Enemies, piles and overflow

Unchanged from v3 except where soldiers touch them.

- **Enemies:** packed arrays and a spatial hash (ADR 0001); 10 HP growing ×1.05 a wave, 2 cells/s, bounty 1; separation, personal space, collision, jam detection, pace, wander and heading offset as in v3, with the two neighbour scans merged (section 5). `Enemies.damage(id, amount, by)` records the last hitter; towers, grinders and soldiers pass their id, a blast passes its bloater's credit and debug kills pass `NONE`. The death list holds each dead enemy's position, kind, killer and blast flag, in removal order.
- **Soldier targets.** Each enemy has a soldier target, `NONE` unless it is attacking a soldier (section 12). An enemy with one holds its ground like an attacker at step 4 and takes no part in a crush: it doesn't count as stalled. It is set and cleared at step 5.
- **The bloater:** 30 HP, 1.6 cells/s, bounty 2, drawn ×1.5 in its own colour, colliding at the normal radius, no body. Where a normal enemy would start attacking a structure, or a soldier, it lights a 1 s fuse and holds its ground without damage; when the fuse runs out it dies with no killer. Killing it during the fuse blows it up there, credited to its killer. Starting a tumble puts its fuse out.
- **Blasts** resolve at the removal step, breadth-first through chains: every living enemy within 2 cells of a blast's centre dies as a blast kill credited to the bloater's killer and leaves no body; every building with a cell centre within 2 cells takes 30 once per blast, as does the base, after every kill in the chain is credited; every pile and wall with its cell centre within 2 cells is erased; and, from v4, **every living soldier within 2 cells takes 30 once per blast** (section 12). The edge is hard.
- **Piles and walls:** five levels (15/30/45/60% slow, then a 15 HP wall); bodies land on the cell their enemy died on or the nearest valid cell from the death position; a broken wall drops to a level-3 pile; decay is off (both chances 0); `Piles.erase(cell)` clears a cell, and only a blast erases. A tumbling death, or one within 1 s of landing, falls back to where its tumble started. A dead soldier's body lands by the same rule (section 14).
- **Overflow:** barriers (connected wall and building cells, 8-way) relabelled on change, the 3-cell band, progress and stalled enemies, the gauge (fill 1.5 per stalled enemy per second, leak 40, clamp 0–120, active above 10 until below 4), tiers (trickle 20, stream 50, surge 90) launching 0.5, 3 or 15 tumbles a second from the front rank with 2 relief each, tumbles planned straight over the touched barrier cell facing the base (else along the direct field's parent chain) to the first free cell with fewer than 6 enemies, moving at 180% raised and shootable, grinders hurting per cell crossed, and landing back on the sensible field. Soldiers are never part of a barrier and don't count towards a landing cell's crowd, and a tumbling enemy ignores them. A tumble that crosses a wall cell records it with its landing cell for the knock-off (section 13).

## 8. Buildings, power, repair, towers and grinders

Unchanged from v3, with the barracks joining the buildings.

| Kind | Footprint | Cost | HP | Power area | Hotkey |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tower | 2×2 | 120 | 60 | 4 | T |
| Grinder | 2×2 | 90 | 100 | 2 | G |
| Pylon | 1×1 | 30 | 20 | 10 | P |
| Repair yard | 2×2 | 120 | 60 | 4 | R |
| **Barracks** | 2×2 | 200 | 100 | 3 | **B** |
| Base (not a building) | 4×4 | – | 200 | 10 | – |

- **Power:** square power areas from the footprint's edge (Chebyshev), powered through overlaps outwards from the always-powered base, recomputed whenever the set of buildings changes. An unpowered building stops working but stays a structure.
- **Working:** powered, and not under repair unless it is a pylon. `Buildings.is_working(id)` is the one check towers, grinders, drones and barracks read. A building is under repair while a drone is repairing it, not while the drone flies there.
- **Placement (step 0):** every footprint cell in bounds, not no-build, on the power grid and clear (no rock, base, building or pile), then the barracks limit for a barracks, then gold. Rejections: "off the map", "too close to a spawn edge", "off the power grid", "occupied", **"barracks limit reached"** or "not enough gold". Soldiers don't block placement: a building placed over soldiers pushes them off (section 10). Building is allowed during waves, in the build phase and while paused. No selling or demolishing.
- **Repair yards:** one drone each, `HOME → OUTBOUND → REPAIRING → RETURNING`, straight flight at 8 cells/s, 5 HP/s until full, repair area 8, a threshold of 25/50/75/100% (50% when built), one assignment, auto picking nearest the yard then lowest HP then lowest id, preferring a building no other drone targets. Drones repair buildings only (barracks included), never soldiers, walls or the base, and only during waves.
- **Towers:** 5 damage, 3.5 shots/s, range 8 from the footprint's centre, instant hits, firing in build order, cooldown in ticks, ready when built, targeting per tower (`bloaters_first` by default: the bloater in range closest to the base by the sensible field, else the enemy in range closest to the base; or `closest_to_base`).
- **Grinders:** 3 HP/s to every enemy touching them (within `separation_radius + wall_reach` of a cell's square), 3 at once per grinder cell a tumbler enters, kill credit and their own combo. **Grinders never hurt soldiers.**

## 9. Barracks and training

Sources: [Getting units: the armoury, cost, training and cap](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/162), [v4 starting numbers: soldier and barracks](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/180).

- **A building under every rule** (section 8): placed on the grid, powering an area of 3, attacked when in the way, part of a barrier, repaired by drones, destroyed leaving nothing.
- **Barracks limit:** at most `barracks.limit` (5) at once. A destroyed barracks frees its place. The build button shows the count ("3/5") and greys out at the limit.
- **Capacity** = `barracks.capacity` (10) × the barracks standing, unpowered ones included. **Used** = living soldiers + every queued soldier. Soldiers don't belong to a barracks: a destroyed barracks only lowers capacity and no soldier dies with it. A player over capacity keeps every soldier but can't train until under it. Soldiers added by the debug command count towards used.
- **Training.** `Train(barracks_id, count)` queues `count` soldiers (1, or 5 for shift-click), as many as fit in the queue (5), capacity and gold allow, paying 30 gold each on queuing. A Train that can queue none is rejected with the reason ("the queue is full", "capacity is full" or "not enough gold"). `CancelTraining(barracks_id, slot)` removes that slot and refunds it.
  - **In the build phase** training is instant: a queued soldier appears at step 0 of the tick it is queued.
  - **During a wave** each barracks trains its queue's head over `training_time` (5 s, 300 ticks), at step 2, only while it is working; unpowered or under repair, the timer pauses and resumes where it left off.
  - **At wave end** every barracks' queue trains at once, in build order, since the build phase has begun.
  - **Destroyed:** the whole queue is refunded at step 9; only the building's own gold is lost.
- **Where a new soldier appears:** the free cell in the ring around the barracks' footprint whose centre is nearest the base's centre (ties to the lowest cell index), else the nearest free cell outwards by a breadth-first search through cells a soldier can stand on. A cell is **free** when a soldier can stand on it (not rock, base or a building) and no soldier's spot is on it. The same rule places debug soldiers (from the base's ring) and soldiers pushed off a new building's footprint.
- **Rally point:** `SetRallyPoint(barracks_id, cell)` on a cell a soldier can stand on (else the nearest such cell), during a wave, in the build phase or while paused; `ClearRallyPoint` removes it. Each new soldier from that barracks gets a single-soldier Move order to it in Cluster formation, so new soldiers gather around it (section 11). Without one, a new soldier stands where it appears.
- Each barracks reports its status (Unpowered, else Under repair, else Training or Idle), its queue, the head's time left and its rally point, for the details.

## 10. Soldiers and how they move

Sources: [What the player controls: squad or soldier, and which commands](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/160), [How units move among enemies, piles and walls](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/163), [v4 starting numbers: soldier and barracks](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/180).

### Storage

`Soldiers` keeps packed arrays, swap-removed at step 9 with an id-to-index map, as `Enemies` does: id, position, HP, **spot** (where a standing soldier holds), **order** (none, or the field and slot it is walking to), path cursor, targeting mode, fire cooldown, current target, kills this run, and a flag for a blast kill. Elevation isn't stored: it is read from the level of the cell under the soldier (section 13). Soldiers are added at the end in id order, so iterating them is deterministic.

### Standing and walking

- **Standing** (no order): the soldier holds its spot. Pushed more than 0.1 cells off it, it walks back at its normal speed; more than 1 cell off (a knock-off, or a long shove), it plans a path back as for a Move (section 11).
- **Walking** (an order): it steers to the next cell on its path at `speed` (2.5 cells/s) × its cell's slow: 15/30/45/60% on a pile, 75% (`wall_climb_slow`) on a wall. On a tick when it had a target in range at the end of the last tick, it walks at half that (section 12). On reaching its slot, the order ends and the slot becomes its spot.
- **Solid to soldiers:** buildings, rock, the base and the map's edge. A soldier resolves collision against their cell squares by the enemies' rule at its body radius (`separation_radius`). Walls and piles are not solid to soldiers. The no-build margin is open ground.

### Soldiers among enemies (soft collision by mass)

At step 5, for each soldier in order, every living, non-tumbling enemy in the enemies' 3×3 buckets around it whose centre is closer than two body radii is pushed apart along the line between them. The overlap closes by mass: the enemy takes `mass / (mass + 1)` of it (5/6) and the soldier `1 / (mass + 1)` (1/6). Each enemy pushed is then resolved against walls and solids as in its own collision step. The pass runs from the soldier side and reads the hash built at step 3, so the enemy loop gains no work per enemy.

- A standing soldier pushed off its spot walks back (above), so a soldier line holds against a trickle, bends under a stream and gives way to a surge.
- A walking soldier is pushed back by the crowd ahead of it, so it shoulders through slowed by how packed it is.
- Enemies don't separate from soldiers in their own scan; this pass is their only contact.

### Among soldiers

After the enemy pass, each pair of soldiers closer than `personal_space_radius` (0.7) is pushed apart softly by `personal_space_push`, all pairs at once (pushes averaged, as for enemies). A standing soldier takes no push from a walking one; the walking one takes it all. Two standing soldiers don't push each other: their spots are a cell apart.

### Building on soldiers

When a building is placed (step 0), every soldier whose position is in its footprint moves to the nearest free cell by the new-soldier rule (section 9). A standing soldier's spot moves with it; a walking soldier keeps its order and its path is planned again.

## 11. Orders, pathing and formations

Sources: [What the player controls: squad or soldier, and which commands](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/160), [Directing units: selection and command feel](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/161), [How units move among enemies, piles and walls](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/163), [The unit in the HUD: details and command card for selected soldiers](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/170).

### Orders

- **Move(ids, target, formation)** gives every living soldier in `ids` one shared order: an anchor, a set of slots in the formation, a field to path on, and each soldier's slot. It replaces any order those soldiers had. Soldiers already sent by an earlier order keep it, so one group can be sent in a Line and the next in a Cluster.
- **Stop(ids)** ends their orders: each stands where it is, and that becomes its spot.
- Orders are taken during a wave, in the build phase and while paused (the paused drain, section 2). There is no hold, attack-move, chase, focus fire or waypoint queue: right-click on an enemy is a Move to the ground under it.

### The anchor

The anchor is the target's cell if a soldier can stand on it and reach it, else the nearest cell (by straight distance, ties to the lowest index) that a soldier of the order can stand on and reach. Reach is read from **walkable regions**: `UnitPaths` labels the connected regions of cells a soldier can stand on (8-way, no corner cutting past solid cells), relabelled when a building is built or destroyed. A soldier in a region the anchor isn't in can't reach it and stops.

### Paths: one field per order

- `UnitPaths.plan` starts a Dijkstra field outwards from the anchor over cells a soldier can stand on: octile steps with no corner cutting past solid cells, each step costing its length ÷ the speed factor of the cell entered (1 on clear ground, 0.85–0.4 on a pile, 0.25 on a wall). It stops once it has settled every soldier's cell and every slot (or run out of reachable cells).
- **The budget:** every field in flight shares one budget of `PATH_SETTLE_CELLS` settled cells a tick, chosen so it costs about 0.5 ms on the M5, oldest field first. A short order settles in a tick; one across the map, about 3,400 cells, in a handful. A soldier whose field isn't ready yet shows as Moving and stands until it is. The budget is a cell count, so replays are exact.
- **A soldier's path** runs through the field's shortest-path tree: up its own parent chain towards the anchor until it meets a cell on its slot's chain, then down the slot's chain to the slot. It is derived once per plan, so walking it costs nothing per tick beyond steering.
- **Replanning:** at step 1, a changed cell that the field has already settled, at or under the highest value among its walking soldiers and slots, restarts that field under the budget. The soldiers keep following the old paths until the new field is ready, then take their new paths. A slot a soldier can no longer stand on moves to the nearest free slot cell. Nothing else replans a path.
- A field is dropped once none of its soldiers is still walking. Rally fields (section 9) live while their rally point stands, replanned the same way.

This fixes the control prototype's soldiers getting stuck behind a wall's corner, and never touches the enemies' flow fields, which lead only to the base.

### Formations

The formation is one army-wide setting on the command card, kept by the view and sent with each Move. Slots are chosen at order time:

- **Cluster** (the default): the N cells nearest the anchor in the order's field (lowest field value, ties to the lowest index) that a soldier can stand on and no standing soldier holds, about one soldier per cell, so the cluster wraps round corners rather than through them.
- **Line:** centred on the anchor, set across the direction of travel (the perpendicular to the line from the selection's mean position to the target). Slots step out 1 cell at a time along it, alternating sides from the centre, taking the cell under each step and skipping cells already taken by this line or held by a standing soldier. A side ends at the first cell a soldier can't stand on or the field can't reach, or at the map's edge, and the rest go on the other side; once both sides have ended, a second rank forms one cell behind, the same way.
- **Assigning slots:** soldiers take slots one by one, nearest the anchor first by field value (ties to the lowest id), each taking the free slot nearest it by straight distance.
- Each soldier walks its own path to its slot. The shape forms on arrival, not on the march.

### Rally orders

A new soldier from a barracks with a rally point gets a one-soldier Move to it in Cluster formation, on that barracks' rally field, so it takes the nearest free cell around the rally point. It can be stopped or re-ordered like any Move.

## 12. Fighting

Sources: [How units fight and are fought](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/164), [Units and the economy: bounty, combo and upkeep](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/175), [v4 starting numbers: soldier and barracks](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/180).

### Soldiers fire (step 7)

- By the tower's rules with the soldier's own numbers: one target at a time, instant hits, no line of sight, a cooldown in ticks, ready when trained. 2 damage at 2 shots/s, range 4 cells from the soldier's position plus 0.5 a level of elevation (section 13). Targeting per soldier by `SetSoldierTargeting`, `bloaters_first` by default, through `Enemies.target_in_range` as for a tower. A soldier keeps its target while it is alive and in range, else picks again.
- Soldiers fire standing or walking. A soldier that has a target at the end of step 7 walks at half speed on the next tick.
- Each soldier counts its kills this run.

### Enemies attack soldiers (step 5)

- **In the way:** after the contact pass, an enemy that is pressed against a soldier (centres within two body radii plus `wall_reach`) and jammed (its jam counter at `jam_seconds`, as for a structure) gets that soldier as its soldier target, unless it already attacks a structure, is tumbling, or the soldier stands on a wall. Enemies never leave their route for a soldier.
- **Attacking:** each enemy with a soldier target deals `structure_damage` (2 HP/s, 2/60 a tick) to it at step 5, and holds its ground at step 4. The target clears when the soldier dies, moves out of reach or climbs onto a wall.
- **Safe on a wall:** a soldier at elevation 5 is never an enemy's soldier target, since an enemy jammed against the wall is held up by the wall.
- **Bloaters:** where a normal enemy would take a soldier target, a bloater lights its fuse instead, as at a structure. A bloater that blows itself up on a soldier is credited to nobody: no bounty, and its blast kills pay ×1.
- **Blasts:** at step 9, every living soldier whose position is within the blast radius (2 cells) of a blast's centre takes `blast_damage` (30) once per blast, after the chain's kills are credited. A soldier at 0 HP from a blast is marked as a blast kill. Two blasts kill a soldier (45 HP).
- **Grinders** never hurt soldiers. **Tumbling enemies** ignore soldiers while tumbling; one that lands against a soldier attacks it by the rule above.

### Bounty and combo

- A soldier's kill pays the normal bounty (1, or 2 for a bloater) × the soldier's tier, through the same death list and `RunState.pay`: the soldier's id is the killer.
- Each soldier builds its own combo by the building rules and v3 numbers (2 s timer, ×2 from 10 kills, ×3 from 25, cap ×3). Moving never breaks it, only its timer; a dead soldier's combo is removed with it, at step 9 before paying, so a kill it made on the tick it died pays ×1.
- A soldier that kills a bloater gets every kill its blast makes, chained blasts included, as a building does.
- No upkeep: a fielded soldier costs nothing beyond its training price.

## 13. Elevation

Sources: [High ground](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/165).

- **Elevation** is `Piles.levels` at the soldier's cell: 0 on the ground, 1–4 on a pile, 5 on a wall. It follows the cell with nothing stored: walking, a pile growing under the soldier (lifted on top), a blast erasing it (0), a wall destroyed under it (3).
- **Range:** `range + range_per_level × elevation`, from the soldier's own elevation only, never the target's: 4 on the ground, 5 on a level-2 pile, 6.5 on a wall. It is the only bonus. A step down that leaves the current target out of range drops it, and the soldier picks again.
- **Knock-off (step 5):** for each wall cell a tumble crossed this tick (`Overflow.take_knock_offs()`), every soldier whose position is on that cell is moved to the nearest free ground cell (level 0, not rock, base or a building) to that tumble's landing cell, by a breadth-first search from the landing cell that doesn't pass through wall cells, so it never crosses back over the wall. With no ground that side, it lands on the landing cell itself, as the tumbler does. Its spot doesn't change, so a standing soldier that survives plans its way back (section 10). Soldiers on other cells of the wall stay.
- **Enemies** get nothing from height.

## 14. Death, bodies and recovery

Sources: [Unit death, bodies and healing](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/166).

- **Death (step 9):** after blasts, every soldier at 0 HP is removed. Its combo is removed, its place in capacity is freed at once (used drops), and nothing replaces it. `soldiers_died` reports its id and position; the view drops it from the selection and every control group and plays a brief death effect where it fell.
- **Body:** a dead soldier queues one body at its position by the body pile rule, unless a blast killed it, in which case it leaves none, so the crater stays clear. Once landed, it is a body like any other.
- **Kills** counts enemy deaths only; a dead soldier pays nothing and isn't a kill.
- **Recovery (step 10):** on wave end, every living soldier returns to full HP. Nothing heals a soldier during a wave. A newly trained soldier starts at full HP.

## 15. Waves, run state and combos

Unchanged from v3.

- **Waves:** "next wave" starts the next one; wave size `round(20 × 1.25^(n−1))` (about 149 at wave 10, 455 at 15, 1,390 at 20), HP growth ×1.05, one or two of the map's spawn edges, clump bursts of 16 at an average of 10 a second. **Bloaters** from wave 5: a base count of 1 + (wave − 5) ÷ 6 (rounded down), and a roll fixed per run and wave for one fewer (25%) or one more (10%), at random slots among the bursts, taking normal enemies' places. Enemies left = wave size − kills this wave; in the build phase it shows the next wave's size and bloaters. Wave tail: Enemies left ≤ 10 + 2 × the wave number. Wave end: every enemy spawned and none alive. Soldiers don't count towards any of it.
- **Run state:** base HP 200 and gold 300 at the start; at 0 base HP the run ends, `game_over` fires and only a restart is accepted. Kills counts every enemy death in the run.
- **Combos** per killer (building or soldier): count, tier, a 2 s timer and best this run. A credited kill adds 1, sets the tier (×1 below 10, ×2 from 10, ×3 from 25, cap ×3) and restarts the timer; a timer that runs out breaks the combo at step 9, before paying. Each death pays its kind's bounty × its killer's tier after counting that kill; a death with no killer, or whose killer was destroyed or died this tick, pays ×1 and feeds no combo. `payout(amount, tier)` fires once a tick when any kill paid above ×1, and `combo_events` reports each kill pulse, tier-up and break.

## 16. View

The v3 view stands: plain coloured shapes from one committed atlas, one y-sorted world, pooled enemy canvas items, pile lift, hit flash, tracers, drones, zoom, UI scaling, the bottom HUD (status, details, command card, toasts above the bar), combo rings, pressure gauges and blast rings. v4 adds:

### Drawing

- **Atlas:** the soldier (one figure per soldier), the barracks, the selection marker, the move marker, the rally flag and a death effect, drawn by `tools/make_atlas.gd`.
- **Soldiers** are pooled canvas items in the y-sorted world, read from `Soldiers` each frame, lifted by their cell's pile level as bodies are, so a soldier on a wall stands on top of it.
- **Selection marker:** drawn above everything (piles and the crowd never hide it), on a layer over the world. Its final shape is left to the art.
- **Move marker:** a short flash where a Move order lands.
- **Combo rings** above soldiers with a combo, as above buildings, hidden at ×1.
- **Tracers** from soldiers' shots, from the soldier's position.
- **Death effect:** brief, where a soldier fell. No alert.
- **Selected soldiers' range:** each soldier's range circle at its own elevation, drawn as one merged shape with a faint fill and a single outline, in the colour of a tower's range circle, following the soldiers as they move and climb. The circles are drawn as solid shapes into a mask, and the mask is drawn with a fill-and-edge shader, so the union costs one pass whatever the count.
- **Rally point:** a flag and a line from the barracks while it is selected.

### Input

- **Selecting soldiers:** click a soldier to select it alone; left-drag a box to select every soldier inside it, never buildings; shift-click or shift-drag adds or removes. Clicking a building or the base selects it and clears selected soldiers. Esc or a click on empty ground clears. A box select doesn't start while a building is being placed.
- **Orders:** right-click on the ground or an enemy with soldiers selected sends `Move` with the card's Formation; with a barracks selected it sends `SetRallyPoint`; while placing a building it cancels, as now.
- **Control groups:** Ctrl+1–9 saves the selected soldiers, 1–9 selects that group again, and pressing the number twice within a short double-press time centres the camera on the group's mean position. Groups and the selection drop soldiers that die.
- **Camera:** a left-drag is the selection box, so it no longer pans. Panning is a middle-drag, a two-finger trackpad drag or the arrow keys. Zoom is unchanged.
- **Pause:** Space toggles `SetPaused`. HUD buttons don't take keyboard focus, so Space always reaches the pause, never a focused "Next wave". While paused, every command drains at once (section 2).
- Build hotkeys (T, G, P, R, B) still work with soldiers selected, and using one clears the soldier selection.

### HUD

- **Status:** **Capacity 12/50** (used/capacity) beside gold, from the first barracks on.
- **Command card, build menu:** Tower, Grinder, Pylon, Repair yard and **Barracks** (with its cost, hotkey B and the count, "3/5", greyed at the limit), and Next wave.
- **Command card, soldiers selected:** swaps to **Stop** (S), **targeting** (A) and **Formation** (F) on the top row, with Next wave in its usual slot, and swaps back when the selection clears. Targeting and Formation are each one button that flips between their two states and shows the current one. When the selected soldiers' modes differ, targeting shows **Mixed** with neither pressed, and a click or A sets them all, from Mixed to Bloaters first. Formation is the view's one army-wide setting, Cluster at launch.
- **One soldier's details:** HP and max HP, status (**Moving** while under an order, else Firing or Idle), damage, shots/s, range with the elevation bonus apart ("Range 6 (+2 high ground)"), kills this run, and its combo ring as a building's.
- **Several soldiers' details:** "N soldiers", their summed kills this run, and a grid of small tiles, one per soldier in selection order, coloured by HP. A click on a tile selects that soldier alone; shift-click takes it out. No ring.
- **Barracks details:** status (Unpowered, else Under repair, else Training or Idle), its queue as slots (click to cancel and refund), **Train soldier** (shift-click queues 5; greyed when capacity or the queue is full or gold is short, with the reason on hover) and **Clear rally point**. Selecting it shows its power area.
- Buildings keep their controls in their details in v4.

## 18. Testing, CI and performance

Sources: [Testing, CI and performance checks](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/10), [v4 performance plan](https://github.com/vandelay87/pile-up/issues/158).

### The test seam

- **The Simulation is the seam**, as in v3: build a `Simulation` on a tiny inline map (`TestMaps.from_rows`) with fixed settings and seed, queue commands, run ticks, and assert on public state and end-of-tick signals. Tests set up a situation, as a player or the debug panel could, and check what comes out.
- New public state to assert on: soldiers' positions, spots, HP, elevation, orders, slots and paths; capacity and used; each barracks' queue, timer and rally point; gold paid and refunded; enemies' soldier targets; which soldiers a blast hit; soldier deaths and their bodies; kills credited to a soldier and its combo; the fields' state while fields are pending and after they swap.
- **Kept seams:** the routing exactness test stays at `Routing` and now checks the field after budgeted settling swaps it in; `bench_sim` and the local benchmarks stay the performance seam.
- Tests whose step numbers change with the new steps are updated by the ticket that adds them. v3 tests the v4 rules change (left-drag panning, the paused queue) are rewritten by the ticket that changes the rule.
- Each build ticket's acceptance criteria name the tests it adds, written test-first. Rendering, input and UI (selection, the box, the marker, control groups, the camera, the card, details, the merged range, flags, effects) have no automated tests; the human checks them in playtests.

### The step profiler

- **It comes into the build** as `tools/bench_profile.tscn`, built on the existing `Simulation.step_observer`: it times each tick step from the observer's calls and prints per-step mean, p99, max, share and mean inside the worst 1% of ticks, plus the whole tick. It takes the crowd from `BenchCrowd`, so it runs the 1,000 dense case, the 1,390 dense case and, with an option, the real wave 20.
- **The per-system timing marks** from `research/profile-1000-tick` (`Prof.add` inside `Enemies.move`, `Routing` and `Overflow`) stay on that branch: they sit inside hot loops, and the step-level times answer what v4 needs, since units have steps of their own. A ticket that needs the inside of a step adds marks on a throwaway branch, as the profile did.

### The unit budget at a full army

- **The army in the benches:** `BenchCrowd` gains an army of 50 soldiers (from `AddSoldiers`) standing in and around its crowd ring, some on walls it raises and some on the ground, and every 120 ticks it sends ten of them on a Move across the ring through the crowd, alternating Cluster and Line. `bench_sim`, `bench_frame` and `bench_profile` all take it, from the ticket that puts soldiers among enemies.
- **The check:** on the M5, release build, `bench_profile` at 1,000 dense with and without the army. The army passes when the tick p99 with it is no more than about 2 ms over the tick p99 without it, and no more than about 14 ms in all; the two unit steps' p99 show where any overrun sits. Every ticket that touches soldiers, pathing, enemies or the tick driver pastes both tables in its PR's Performance section.
- **In CI:** runners differ by about 2×, so CI can't check an absolute 2 ms. `sim-budget` keeps its relative gate (the PR's best tick mean at most 1.2× its base's on the same runner, with bench_sim's 80 ms backstop), and once the army is in `bench_sim`, each later unit ticket is compared against a base that already fields it.

### CI

CI keeps `lint`, `test`, `sim-budget` and `export`, all required on every PR. `bench_sim.gd` grows to include the army walking through the crowd and fighting it, alongside v3's cases (a crush overflowing at surge, grinders under it, a bloater chain erasing a wall, drones repairing, power cut and restored), and checks that soldiers fired and were attacked. Its limit changes only in a PR that explains why.

- **v4's question:** do soldiers the player directs give a satisfying answer to leaks and floods, without turning pile-up into an RTS?
- **Playtest questions:**

  - The soldier and barracks numbers as a whole: one soldier cheap and weak, a handful holding a breach, piles worth standing on without being a must, two blasts to kill a soldier.
